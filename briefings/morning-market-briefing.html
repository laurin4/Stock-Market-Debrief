Morning Market Briefing

Inflation reset the rate path. The global equity fund is still far above its franc alerts.

Wednesday 11 November 2026 · 07:30 Europe/Zurich · For a long-term holder of a global equity exchange-traded fund (ETF). Figures below are closes unless a clock time is given.

Wall Street fell on Tuesday after October consumer prices beat forecasts and Nvidia dropped for a second day, Asia’s chip shares bounced before the European open, and the franc value of the London listing of VWRA — the Vanguard FTSE (Financial Times Stock Exchange) All-World accumulating exchange-traded fund — stayed near 154, well clear of the 140 and 135 alerts.

Americas

The Bureau of Labor Statistics said the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers rose 0.4% in October, seasonally adjusted, after 0.6% in September. Economists polled by Reuters had expected 0.3%. The index excluding food and energy rose 0.3% for a third straight month; the Reuters poll had expected 0.2%. Shelter rose 0.3% and accounted for more than 30% of the monthly increase. Food rose 0.5%. Energy rose 0.7% after 5.2% in September, and gasoline rose 1.0% after 9.1%. The Bureau cannot publish an October year-on-year rate for the all-items index because it did not collect October 2025 prices during a lapse in appropriations. In the same table, gasoline’s not-seasonally-adjusted 12-month change was published at 37.4%. The index level for all urban consumers was 337.511 (1982–84 = 100). The Bureau’s release list also said real average hourly earnings were unchanged in October. Reuters reported that annual consumer inflation in September, the last available yearly reading, was 3.7%.

Reuters’ late wrap put the 2-year Treasury yield up 18.44 basis points at 4.731%, its largest one-day jump since the April 2025 tariff selloff, and the 10-year yield up 12.55 basis points at 4.988%. CNBC, earlier in the New York afternoon, had the 10-year back above 5% intraday and last up 13 basis points at 4.992%, the 30-year up more than 9 basis points at 5.352%, and the 2-year up more than 18 basis points at 4.736%. A $42 billion 10-year auction was described as soft. The dollar index, which measures the US dollar against a basket of major currencies, rose 0.56% to 102.25. The euro was down 0.54% at $1.1162, and the dollar was up 0.58% at 158.81 yen.

The Standard & Poor’s 500 fell 29.01 points, or 0.38%, to 7,520.93. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 157.86 points, or 0.30%, to 52,231.89. The Nasdaq Composite fell 187.26 points, or 0.74%, to 25,065.87. Reuters said the MSCI gauge of stocks across the globe — MSCI was formerly Morgan Stanley Capital International — slipped 0.12% to 1,107.66. CNBC said the S&P 500 remained about 4.1% under its record and the Nasdaq about 9.6% under its record. The same session followed a Monday on which Reuters said the Nasdaq had risen 2.7%.

Nvidia was the centre of the equity damage. Reuters said the shares fell sharply for a second day as investors worried about chief executive Jensen Huang’s future after the company opened an internal probe into spy claims. CNBC put the decline at about 5.5% and the market value at about $4.07 trillion, the lowest since late March. After the close, Reuters said CoreWeave beat quarterly revenue estimates and raised its annual forecasts. The same Reuters story said an Anthropic initial public offering was being discussed at a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion, with the roadshow getting underway. Those offering terms are not final.

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller, speaking in London before the numbers, said he would support a quarter-point increase at the 8–9 December meeting unless core consumer prices rose 0.3% or more, in which case a half-point increase should be considered. That 0.3% condition was met. Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said policy, then in a 3.75% to 4.00% range, was adding to inflation and that she thought the central bank needed at least another half a percentage point of increases, possibly faster than one quarter-point step. Reuters said late-afternoon pricing put the chance of a half-point December move near 10%, after about 15% in the morning, and that traders had shifted toward three quarter-point increases by mid-2027 rather than two. CNBC, citing the CME Group FedWatch tool, said the chance of a quarter-point increase next month had risen to about 90%. Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman said inflation was not on a downward trend, and she also warned that falling share prices and higher bond yields could hurt the economy. She did not sound in a hurry.

CNBC reported that the US bond market is closed today for Veterans Day. Equity futures were still being quoted overnight: as of 22:58 Eastern Time on Tuesday (04:58 in Zurich on Wednesday), CNBC had Dow futures up about 88 points, or 0.2%, S&P 500 futures up 0.2%, and Nasdaq-100 futures up 0.4%. That is not a 07:30 print.

Europe and Switzerland

European cash markets were still shut at 07:30 in Zurich. Tuesday’s closes, from Deutsche Presse-Agentur via Yahoo Finance: London’s FTSE 100 (Financial Times Stock Exchange 100) fell 31.28 points, or 0.29%, to 10,751.59; Germany’s DAX (Deutscher Aktienindex) added 80.26 points, or 0.31%, to 26,347.45; France’s CAC 40 gained 45.30 points, or 0.55%, to 8,278.59; Switzerland’s Swiss Market Index rose 91.79 points, or 0.61%, to 15,037.94. Reuters said the STOXX Europe 600 rose about 0.3%. Gains had been larger before the US inflation report. France’s unemployment rate fell to 8.2% in the third quarter from 8.3%, against an expected rise to 8.4%.

Armistice Day is a public holiday in France. Le Revenu reports that Euronext Paris is nevertheless scheduled to trade today; 11 November is not on its 2026 closure list. Germany’s Federal Statistical Office is due to publish final October consumer prices today. Its releases are generally issued at 08:00 Central European Time, so the final print was not out at briefing time. The Swiss National Bank’s market-operations page lists two Confederation bond auction announcements at 10:30 today, for securities CH1544304186 and CH1544304178. No size or coupon was shown on that page.

London trading in VWRA, the Vanguard FTSE All-World UCITS (Undertakings for Collective Investment in Transferable Securities) ETF, US-dollar accumulating share class, starts at 08:00 Greenwich Mean Time, which is 09:00 in Zurich. There is no Wednesday price yet.

Asia and commodities

A Dow Jones desk note timestamped 06:55 in Zurich described a split session: chip shares higher in Korea, Taiwan and Japan, Chinese technology shares lower. These are last indications from that note, not official closing prints, except Sydney, whose cash close was already listed. The S&P/ASX 200 was shown at 9,153.20, up 0.3%. The Nikkei 225 was shown at 61,257.14, up 1.3%, with the cash close due around 07:00 Zurich. Korea’s Kospi was shown at 5,357.82, up 2.3%, into a close due at 07:30. Hong Kong’s Hang Seng was shown at 24,343.48, down 0.9%, with that market open until 09:00 Zurich. The Shanghai Composite was shown at 3,659.99, down 0.1%, open until 08:00. Taiwan’s Taiex was described as up 2.9%, with Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing up 4.2%. SK Hynix was described as up 5.3% and Samsung Electronics up 2.9%.

SoftBank reported a quarterly loss. CNBC put the fiscal second-quarter net loss at 1.12 trillion yen, about $7 billion, and had the shares down about 3% in an earlier update. Dow Jones put the loss at $7.1 billion, the first negative quarter in almost two years, largely from power-generation equipment tied to US artificial-intelligence data centres, and said the shares had been down more than 10% before recovering to about 0.1% higher by the time of the 06:55 note. Dow Jones also said CoreWeave’s results were supporting the regional chip trade, and that Chinese internet shares (NetEase down 1.3%, Meituan down 3.2% in that note) were weighing a higher US rate path and a Trump–Xi meeting on 18–19 November.

Reuters said Brent crude settled $1.08, or 1.2%, lower at $91.49 a barrel, and US West Texas Intermediate settled 81 cents, or 0.9%, lower at $86.04. Both were the lowest settlements since 31 August, after US officials played down the need for further military action against Iran. Spot gold fell 2.62% to $4,030.56 an ounce. By 06:55 Zurich, Dow Jones indications — which the desk marks as not guaranteed — had Brent at $92.20, up 0.8% from $91.49, West Texas Intermediate at $86.41, up 0.4%, and gold at $4,055, up 0.6% from a prior level the desk listed at $4,030.93. That gold prior is a few tenths away from the Reuters spot close, so the morning gold figure should not be read as a continuation of the same print. The same desk had the euro at $1.1166 and dollar-yen at 158.74.

Long-term ETF lens

VWRA is one global equity holding. Tuesday’s inflation print matters for it because it changes the discount rate, not because any one company missed a quarter. A 10-year Treasury yield just under 5%, a policy rate the Dallas Fed president called too low at 3.75% to 4.00%, and an open debate between a quarter-point and a half-point December step are the macro facts a long-horizon buyer actually lives with. Oil at a two-month low takes some pressure off the next headline inflation print. It did not take pressure off October’s core rate, which was 0.3% for a third month. Reuters quoted Brian Jacobsen of Annex Wealth Management saying the oil shock had already seeped into core prices.

On the fund itself, Yahoo Finance showed the London close on Tuesday at $186.90, down $0.64, or 0.34%, at 18:30:01 Greenwich Mean Time. Yahoo’s page also showed the share about 9.5% higher in 2026 to date and about 11.9% higher over one year, and about 3.4% lower over one month. The Swiss-franc listing on the SIX Swiss Exchange is a different price and is not used here. The franc figure below is the London dollar price multiplied by the dollar-franc rate.

Nothing in this session changes a savings plan that buys the whole market on a schedule. It does argue against treating a one-day chip bounce in Seoul as information about the next decade of global earnings.

Hottest tactical

The liveliest tape into the European open is Asian semiconductors, not the Swiss index. Dow Jones had the Kospi indication up 2.3%, Taiwan up 2.9%, and the Nikkei indication up 1.3%, led by memory and foundry names, after CoreWeave’s after-hours beat. That bounce sits on top of a 5.5% drop in Nvidia itself and a US bond market that cannot clear cash Treasuries today because of the holiday. A tactical trader is long the overnight chip recovery and short the idea that Tuesday’s rate repricing is finished. A VWRA holder does not need a view. The position already owns the chip complex and the rest of the world in the index weights, which this briefing does not restate because the latest fund weight was not pulled from a primary filing.

Biggest risk

The risk that can reprice the whole fund is a December Federal Reserve hike that is larger, or more clearly the start of a series, than markets settled on late Tuesday. Waller’s stated test for considering a half-point move was a core monthly print of 0.3% or higher, and the Bureau of Labor Statistics delivered 0.3%. The 2-year yield’s 18 basis-point jump is the market admitting that test matters. A second, narrower risk is idiosyncratic: Reuters’ report of an internal Nvidia probe. Nvidia is large inside global equity indexes. A governance shock there moves VWRA even if the world economy does not. Neither risk is a forecast that the fund should be sold.

Fact versus speculation

Fact

October consumer prices rose 0.4%, and core prices rose 0.3%. There is no official October year-on-year all-items rate. The S&P 500 closed at 7,520.93, the 10-year yield was 4.988% in the Reuters wrap, Brent settled at $91.49, and spot gold at $4,030.56. VWRA closed at $186.90. The dollar-franc rate on Yahoo at 06:30 GMT was 0.8266. The Swiss Market Index closed Tuesday at 15,037.94.

Speculation

Whether December is a quarter-point or a half-point hike, and whether two or three quarter-point steps follow by mid-2027. Whether the Nvidia probe changes leadership or is a short-lived headline. Whether the Wednesday morning bounce in Brent, gold and Asian chips survives the European and US cash sessions. The Anthropic valuation of “up to $1.4 trillion” is how Reuters described the discussion, not a cleared price. Dow Jones’s 06:55 index levels can still change before those markets close.
