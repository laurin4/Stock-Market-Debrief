Americas

Monday was a broad rebound. Reuters reported the Dow Jones Industrial Average up 1,104.02 points, or 2.15%, to 52,389.75; the Standard & Poor’s 500 up 173.08 points, or 2.35%, to 7,549.94; and the Nasdaq Composite up 673.71 points, or 2.74%, to 25,253.14. CNBC called the Standard & Poor’s 500 move its best day since 8 April. Reuters said that index was rebounding from its biggest weekly drop since April 2025. After the rally, CNBC noted, the Dow was back above its Election Day close, while the Standard & Poor’s 500 and the Nasdaq were still slightly below theirs.

The bounce was broad in semiconductors and narrow at the top of that group. A semiconductor index rose 6.9% after falling more than 12% last week, and Nvidia was the only name in that index to fall. Nvidia, described by Reuters as the world’s second-most valuable company, dropped 4.2% to its lowest close since 7 April. The drop followed an internal investigation into whether a foreign intelligence effort had targeted chief executive Jensen Huang. Investors were, in Reuters’ wording, questioning his future at the firm. That is a governance headline, not a published finding that he will leave.

The macro tape under the rally is still tight. Friday’s employment report showed employers added 165,000 jobs in October, which Reuters called a blowout. CNBC reported the unemployment rate at 4.1%. The yield on the 10-year Treasury note rose 1.48 basis points to 4.874%. Fed funds futures put an 84% chance on a Federal Reserve rate increase in December, up after that jobs report. The Federal Reserve held its benchmark rate in October, CNBC reported, and the next meeting is 8–9 December. The dollar eased on Monday: the euro rose 0.72% to $1.1223, and the dollar fell 0.34% to 157.91 yen.

CNBC, citing the Atlanta Fed, put third-quarter gross domestic product growth at 3.4% and the current-quarter tracking estimate at 3.5%. Early Tuesday, Reuters said futures on the Standard & Poor’s 500 were only slightly higher after Monday’s jump. That futures comment was from the Asian morning, so it is context, not a live New York quote.

Europe and Switzerland

Monday’s pan-European STOXX 600 rose 1.26%, with banks helped by deal talk and results. Credit Agricole proposed that its Italian subsidiary merge with Banco BPM. UniCredit posted a 24% annual rise in third-quarter earnings, ahead of forecasts, and raised its full-year profit goal. MSCI’s global equity gauge rose 2.16% to 1,108.96.

Tuesday’s follow-through was still underway at mid-morning. Reuters put the STOXX 600 up 0.67% at 660.96 as of 09:41 GMT (10:41 Central European Time), within 1% of the record last reached in early August. Benchmark indexes in Germany, France and Italy were each up about 1%. Banks led sectors, up 2.6%. Oil and gas shares were down 1.1%. Associated Press indications around 09:47 UTC had the DAX up 1.3% at 26,267.19, the CAC 40 up 1.2% at 8,233.30, and the FTSE 100 up 0.4% at 10,830.12. Those are open-session readings, not closes.

Infineon rose 6.6% after forecasting revenue growth of more than 20% in 2027 and a profitability increase of about five percentage points, on demand from data centres, power infrastructure and software-defined vehicles. Reuters said Europe’s third-quarter earnings are now expected to rise 25%, versus an earlier estimate near 16%, citing LSEG data, and that the earnings-growth gap with the United States has narrowed to its smallest in more than three years (Deutsche Bank). Lufthansa and Air France-KLM were each up about 1% as fuel costs eased. Reuters also said investors expect the European Central Bank to wait for more data through year-end and possibly raise rates by February. That is anticipation, not a decision.

In Zurich, Monday’s Swiss Market Index close was 14,946.15, according to finanzen.ch. From the 30 December 2025 close of 13,267.48 on the same page, that is a price gain of about 12.7% for 2026 to date. Around 09:15, L’Agefi had the Swiss Market Index up 0.67% at 15,046.87, the Swiss Leader Index up 0.57% at 2,413.90, and the Swiss Performance Index up 0.64% at 21,153.10. Roche, Nestlé and Novartis were only modestly higher, about 0.3% to 0.4%. The 5 November high of 15,092.56, also from finanzen.ch, was still above the morning print. The session had not closed. Local reporters flagged results from PSP Swiss Property, Sunrise, LEM and BKW.

Asia and commodities

Asia closed before Europe opened and took the Monday lead from New York. Reuters said Japan’s Nikkei rose 2.9% and South Korea’s KOSPI rose 4.4%, while MSCI’s broad Asia-Pacific index rose about 2% and moved off the seven-month low hit last week. Yonhap reported the KOSPI close at 5,239.13, up 220.28 points, which matches that 4.4% move. A verified point close for the Nikkei was not in the wire copy used here, so the percentage stands without an index level.

Oil is the other half of the story. Brent crude settled Monday down $5.75, or 5.85%, at $92.57 a barrel. West Texas Intermediate settled down $3.98, or 4.38%, at $86.85. On Tuesday morning Reuters said Brent was down more than 2% and had dipped below $90 for the first time since late August, to about $90 a barrel. President Donald Trump said he was highly unlikely to order fresh attacks on Iran as long as talks progressed, and later that he planned to hold off on new strikes for the time being after appeals from the region, including Qatar’s prime minister. Reuters also reported that both sides had said shipping was hit in or near the Strait of Hormuz. The price is trading a pause in strikes, not a signed peace.

Spot gold was down 0.6% at $4,114.48 an ounce as of 04:30 GMT, after rising more than 1% in the previous session, Reuters reported. December gold futures were up 0.2% at $4,123.50. In the same note, spot silver was down 0.5% at $53.14, platinum was steady at $1,576.44, and palladium was down 0.2% at $1,039.47. Richmond Fed president Tom Barkin said policy should move “with care” and that he had no base case for December. Traders’ 84% December hike probability, via the CME FedWatch tool in that Reuters copy, is the same figure the rates market was carrying after Friday’s jobs data.

Long-term ETF lens

The portfolio anchor in this note is the Vanguard FTSE All-World UCITS ETF (Undertakings for Collective Investment in Transferable Securities), accumulating share class, London listing VWRA, priced in United States dollars. It is not a Swiss-franc share on SIX. A franc figure below is the London dollar price multiplied by the dollar/franc rate. It is a translation for a Swiss holder, not a traded CHF quote.

  • From the 31 December 2025 close of 170.62 dollars to Monday’s close of 187.54 dollars, the share price rose 9.9%. Because this share class accumulates income inside the fund, that price change is the holding-period result in dollars before the franc.
  • Yahoo Finance shows the dollar up 3.60% against the Swiss franc in 2026 to date, and down 1.31% over the past month. An unhedged holder has had a currency tailwind this year and has given a slice of it back recently. Monday’s softer dollar against the euro (up 0.72% to $1.1223) is a reminder that the franc leg and the equity leg do not have to move together.
  • Monday’s 2.16% rise in MSCI’s global gauge, and Tuesday’s firmer European open, repair part of last week’s drop. They do not change a multi-year allocation. The Standard & Poor’s 500 is still a large weight inside a global fund, so United States inflation and the artificial-intelligence complex will move VWRA more than a quiet morning in Roche, Nestlé and Novartis moves the Swiss Market Index.
  • The slow risk is the discount rate. A 10-year yield at 4.874% and an 84% market probability of a December hike mean new savings should still follow the existing contribution rule. They are not a reason to rebuild the portfolio around one morning’s oil move.

Hottest tactical

The live trade is “oil down, equities up,” led by last week’s laggards. European banks were up 2.6% by 10:41 CET, airlines gained about 1%, and Infineon was up 6.6% on a 2027 guide tied to data centres and power. On Monday the semiconductor index jumped 6.9% even as Nvidia fell 4.2%. Reuters quoted a New York investor calling the prior selloff stretched and the buying a mix of short-covering, bargain-hunting and momentum. That is a positioning rebound after a weekly shock tied to the United States midterm elections and worry about artificial-intelligence and data-centre rules. It is not new evidence that those earnings are durable. For a fund that already owns the global market, this is something to watch, not something to chase with a side bet.

Biggest risk

Today’s Consumer Price Index, at 14:30 Central European Time, can reprice the rate path that equities just rallied into. The Dow Jones consensus, reported by CNBC, and the Reuters poll agree: headline prices up 0.3% on the month and core prices, excluding food and energy, up 0.2%. Both would be slower than September’s 0.6% and 0.3%. CNBC, citing the Energy Information Administration, said pump prices fell more than 4% in October, which can cool the headline without settling the rest of the basket. There will be no official year-on-year rate for October, because last year’s federal shutdown left the Bureau of Labor Statistics without a comparable index level. Chicago Fed president Austan Goolsbee said Monday that the problem “has all been on the inflation side.” Bank of America told clients that, unless the monthly print is an “indisputably soft” 0.1%, it doubts the report will stop a December hike. That is a forecast. The fact is that markets have already assigned an 84% probability, so a hot monthly number has less room to be shrugged off, and a soft one is the surprise that would challenge the rally’s rate backdrop.

The second channel is oil. Brent’s slide assumes the pause in strikes lasts. Shipping incidents were still being reported around Hormuz as prices fell. A setback in talks would put the geopolitical premium back into energy, and back into the inflation debate, quickly.

Fact versus speculation

Reported

  • Monday’s three United States index closes, Brent at $92.57 and West Texas Intermediate at $86.85, Nvidia down 4.2%, semiconductors up 6.9%.
  • Tuesday morning levels that are explicitly intraday: STOXX 600 at 660.96 as of 09:41 GMT, Swiss Market Index at 15,046.87 around 09:15, gold at $4,114.48 as of 04:30 GMT, VWRA at 188.72 dollars at 09:47 GMT, dollar/franc at 0.8205 at 10:08 GMT.
  • October payrolls up 165,000. Unemployment rate 4.1%, as reported by CNBC. The 10-year yield at 4.874%. An 84% futures-implied chance of a December hike.
  • The Consumer Price Index is due at 14:30 Central European Time. The published consensus is +0.3% headline and +0.2% core. Officials will not publish an October year-on-year rate.
  • Democrats won control of Congress in last week’s midterm elections, as stated by Reuters.

Not established

  • That talks produce a durable end to the Iran war. The market is pricing a pause in strikes. Shipping incidents were still in the Monday story.
  • That Jensen Huang leaves Nvidia. The share fell on an investigation and on questions about his future. No departure has been reported here.
  • That the Federal Reserve will hike on 8–9 December. Eighty-four percent is a traded probability. Barkin said he had no base case. Bank of America’s “lock in” language is a forecast, as is its interpolated 3.8% headline and 2.7% core annual rates, which are not Bureau of Labor Statistics figures.
  • That the STOXX 600 closes at a record today. At 10:41 CET it was within 1% of the August high, with hours left.
  • That the European Central Bank hikes by February. Reuters described that as what investors anticipate.
