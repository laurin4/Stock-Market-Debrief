Americas

Both New York closes agree. Reuters, in the cash wrap by Stephen Culp and Utkarsh Hathi, and Reuters again in Rodrigo Campos’ later global wrap (Euronext timestamp 23:14 on 17 November), print the same finishes: the Standard & Poor’s 500 (S&P 500) at 7,340.53, down 76.06 points or 1.03%; the Dow Jones Industrial Average at 50,759.42, down 848.75 points or 1.64%; the Nasdaq Composite at 24,328.38, down 181.37 points or 0.74%. Culp writes that the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq posted their first four-day losing streak since September. Campos adds MSCI’s gauge of stocks across the globe at 1,079.50, down 1.21%, and emerging-market stocks down 1.31%, with no level on the emerging-market line.

Breadth was poor. On the New York Stock Exchange, declining issues outnumbered advancers by 3.76 to 1, with 27 new highs and 702 new lows. On the Nasdaq, 1,273 stocks rose and 3,527 fell. The S&P 500 posted 5 new 52-week highs and 10 new lows; the Nasdaq Composite posted 20 new highs and 526 new lows. Volume was 18.90 billion shares, against a 20-day average of 18.81 billion. Culp says every S&P sector fell, with software and services, airlines and banks the clear laggards. Salesforce, Workday, Intuit and Adobe fell between 4.3% and 9.5%. Microsoft eased 2.3%. JPMorgan dipped 2.4% after naming an insider, Bregje de Best, to succeed Jeremy Barnum as chief financial officer in early 2027.

The macro tape was mixed, and the stories do not all use the same adjective. Reuters’ Treasury copy (Caroline Valetkevitch, published by Mint at 02:23 India Standard Time on 18 November, which is 15:53 in New York on the 17th) says Commerce Department retail sales rose 0.2% in October after 0.8% in September, in line with the Reuters poll of 0.2%. The evening Swiss dispatch from AWP described those sales as slightly stronger than expected. This note keeps the Reuters comparison: the 0.2% rise matched the Reuters poll. Import prices “rose more than expected”; no percentage was in the copies read. Industrial output weakened; AWP’s evening currency note said industrial production unexpectedly declined, again without a percentage in the text read. Homebuilder sentiment fell to its lowest level since April 2012 (Culp) and to a more than 14½-year low (the currency and bond stories). St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem, who is not a voter on rates this year, said the Federal Reserve may need several rate increases.

Rate probabilities are snapshots, and they do not match. Culp cites the CME FedWatch tool at a 95.4% likelihood of a 25-basis-point increase in December. AWP rounded the same idea to 95%. Valetkevitch, earlier in the New York afternoon, cites FedWatch at roughly a 98% chance of a 25-basis-point hike at the 8–9 December meeting and about a 2% chance of 50 basis points. Those three figures are left as printed.

Long-term exchange-traded fund lens

The holding this note is written around is the accumulating Vanguard FTSE All-World UCITS ETF (Undertakings for Collective Investment in Transferable Securities; ticker VWRA; ISIN IE00BK5BQT80), listed in Zurich in francs. Vanguard’s page, read for this briefing, stamps the latest prices “at closure 17 Nov 2026”: a net asset value (NAV) of $182.5667, shown as $182.57, down $2.20 or 1.19%, and a Swiss market price of CHF 152.74, down CHF 3.54 or 2.27%. The ongoing charge is 0.14%. Share-class assets are given as $62.19 billion.

The franc price and the dollar NAV are different measurements taken on different clocks. The SIX Swiss Exchange cash close is 17:30 Zurich, which is 11:30 in New York, so Tuesday’s franc print does not include the rest of the United States afternoon. The dollar/franc pair on AWP’s 21:13 read (0.8396, versus 0.8399 the prior evening) did not move enough to explain a 2.27% franc decline against a 1.19% NAV decline. This note does not split the residual into an exact currency effect and an exact timing effect.

Portfolio data on the page are still as at 31 October 2026, not as at Tuesday’s close. The fund held 3,574 stocks against 4,199 in the benchmark. The price/earnings ratio was 19.6 times. The United States was 62.83% of the fund (benchmark 62.71%). Switzerland was 2.08%. The largest holdings were Apple 4.74363%, Nvidia 4.54553%, Microsoft 3.69160%, Amazon 2.21951% and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing 1.68471%. A four-day drop in the S&P 500, a 5% 10-year yield on the afternoon quote, and a governance question at the fund’s second-largest holding are the same risk seen from three places. They are not a reason, on one session, to treat a global accumulating fund as a trade.

Hottest tactical

The tactical event is Nvidia’s first report with Colette Kress as interim chief executive, and it is not yet a cash close. The company said fiscal third-quarter revenue, for the quarter ended 25 October 2026, was $115.8 billion, up 20% from the prior quarter and 103% from a year earlier. Data-center revenue was $107.6 billion, up 21% and 110%. Generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) diluted earnings were $2.70 a share and the non-GAAP figure was $2.68. GAAP and non-GAAP gross margins were 74.8% and 74.9%. The income statement line is $115,844 million, which is the $115.8 billion in the headline.

The outlook, in the company’s words: fourth-quarter revenue of $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, with an extra week in the quarter, and no assumption of data-center compute revenue from China. GAAP and non-GAAP gross margins are expected between 71.0% and 72.0%. Reuters, in the earnings story updated at 16:31 New York, says the $130 billion midpoint compares with an LSEG average estimate of $125.25 billion, and that the shares were up about 2% in extended trading. As of Tuesday’s close the stock was down about 17% this year, and Reuters described the company as worth roughly $3.8 trillion. Culp’s cash-close story, which also caught the early extended trade, says the shares rose by as much as 3%. Campos also says up 2% after hours. The 2% and the “as much as 3%” are both kept. Neither is a Wednesday cash open.

Two governance lines from the same Reuters earnings story sit next to the numbers. Kress is interim chief executive while the board looks at internal and external candidates. Three people told Reuters on Monday that the board is not considering Jensen Huang’s return. Huang said on Tuesday he would be “honored” to return if the board deemed it best. The company also said the board created a committee on national security and export controls, and the release says Nvidia announced an agreement to acquire Hugging Face. The personal allegations around the resignation are under speculation, not here.

The other near-term event is Anthropic, and the wires do not agree on the day. Culp’s close story, after a correction, says the initial public offering was expected on Wednesday and that $1.4 trillion is a valuation target, not a sum to be raised. L’Agefi, quoting John Plassard of Cité Gestion, also puts the listing on Wednesday 18 November. Campos, Ahmed, and AWP’s Swiss close all place the market debut on Thursday. This note does not collapse “Wednesday” and “Thursday” into one timetable.

Biggest risk

For this fund the binding risk is still concentration plus the price of money, not one earnings print. About 63% of VWRA is the United States, and the top of the book is the same artificial-intelligence complex that just fell for a fourth day even as Nvidia’s outlook cleared the published consensus. The afternoon 10-year was still above 5%. Culp quotes a strategist calling the session speculation-driven rather than fact-driven, and records that the Philadelphia Stock Exchange Semiconductor Index’s year-to-date gain has shrunk to about 34% after being as high as 106% in June. One quarter that beats an estimate does not retire the question of who runs Nvidia, or the question of whether a higher Federal Reserve path keeps pressure on a non-yielding and on a richly owned trade. Oil is the second risk, and it cuts both ways: a live war premium in the Strait of Hormuz, and an International Energy Agency surplus as Middle East barrels return. Tuesday’s settlement rose less than a dollar. That is a range, not an all-clear.

Fact versus speculation

Fact, as printed this morning

  • The New York cash closes match across the two Reuters equity stories: S&P 500 7,340.53, Dow 50,759.42, Nasdaq 24,328.38.
  • Nvidia’s release: revenue $115.8 billion, data center $107.6 billion, outlook $130.0 billion plus or minus 3%, no China data-center compute revenue assumed in that outlook. Kress is named interim chief executive in the release.
  • SMI 14,976.04, down 1.43%, all 20 members lower in the AWP close. VWRA Swiss close CHF 152.74. Alerts at 140 and 135 were not hit.
  • Brent and WTI settlements in the Reuters oil story: $93.14 and $86.01. The later $92.97 and $85.73 are a subsequent running quote.
  • October retail sales rose 0.2%, matching the Reuters poll, in the Treasury story.
  • Tokyo Tuesday close 58,860.43. Wednesday morning close 58,696.54. Wednesday’s full close was not in the Nikkei file or the close-story list read here.

Speculation, allegation, or an unresolved quote

  • The account of Huang’s resignation, including reported affairs and an intelligence question about a Chinese businesswoman’s ties, is Reuters reporting based on people familiar with the matter. It is not treated here as an established finding. His comment that he would be honoured to return, and the report that the board is not considering that, are both attributed quotes.
  • FedWatch at 95.4%, about 95%, and about 98% plus a 2% chance of 50 basis points are three afternoon snapshots. They are not a decision. The 10-year at 5.103% is not labelled a Tradeweb 15:00 close.
  • Dollar index 103.44 and 103.52, and euro $1.10133, $1.1008 and $1.1003, are different clocks. FTSE 100 at 10,689.87 and at 10,689.97 is a 0.10-point disagreement between Dow Jones and Reuters.
  • Anthropic on Wednesday versus a Thursday debut, and a $1.4 trillion valuation target, are scheduling and deal talk, not a listing.
  • Deutsche Bank’s leaked-document stories, the Bloomberg report of a BASF approach to Evonik, and the reports that UBS might not win the capital argument or might study a move abroad, are reports. The share moves are the fact.
  • President Trump’s comment, in the Reuters oil story, that the United States is taking seriously the possibility of an Iranian attack involving a so-called dirty bomb, is his statement about a possibility. The drone strike in Bahrain, the Hormuz ship, and the Qeshm airstrikes are the reported events in that copy.
  • A strategist’s hope that Nvidia “recalibrates” the market onto current artificial-intelligence spending is a view. So is the claim that the session was speculation-driven. So is any reading of one beat as the end of the four-day slide.
