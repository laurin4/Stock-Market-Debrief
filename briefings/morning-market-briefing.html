Hottest tactical

The live argument into the New York open is whether Friday was enough. Two futures snapshots, Westbrook’s early mark and the Australian Broadcasting Corporation at 4:00 p.m. Sydney, both showed US contracts higher, on the order of 2% for the broad market and closer to 3% for the Nasdaq complex. That is a futures print on a holiday-thinned week, with Tokyo shut and with no Seoul, Hong Kong or Shanghai close in hand. It is not a cash reversal. In Switzerland on Friday the stocks that rose were the defensives: Nestlé and Novartis among the heavyweights, Swiss Re up 1.4%. In the United States, consumer staples was the only Standard & Poor’s 500 sector in the green. Official buying talk in Seoul, Taipei and the Chinese exchanges is the other tactical prop. Westbrook’s reporting presents those steps as ready if selling continues. They are not evidence that the week’s losses have been retraced.

Biggest risk

For a long-term holder of this one global fund, the risk that matters is treating one violent week as either a reason to abandon the holding or a reason to add because futures are up before the cash open. Almost 63% of the fund is the United States, and the three largest positions sit in the complex that just led the worst week for global stocks since early 2020 on MSCI’s gauge. The 31 October price/earnings ratio of 19.6 times does not describe Friday’s prices, so “already cheap” is not a fact on the page. At the same time, AWP says a December Federal Reserve increase of at least a quarter point is the market’s working assumption, and Campos says a hike is priced, even after Friday’s drop in yields. Those two things can both be true and still leave the December outcome unresolved: no probability was re-read on a FedWatch page this morning. A separate inflation risk sits in the oil headlines. Friday’s settlement rose by less than a dollar, and the weekly path in that story was still described as “on track” lower, while the Hormuz and Red Sea lines remain statements carried from combatants.
