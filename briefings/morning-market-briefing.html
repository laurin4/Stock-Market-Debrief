Americas

At the New York close on Monday 16 November, Reuters reported the Dow Jones Industrial Average at 51,608.17, down 739.38 points, or 1.41 percent; the Standard & Poor’s 500 at 7,416.59, down 145.08 points, or 1.92 percent; and the Nasdaq Composite at 24,509.76, down 626.51 points, or 2.49 percent. The same figures for the Standard & Poor’s 500 appear as the 16 November close on the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis FRED page. CNBC said the Standard & Poor’s 500 and the Nasdaq Composite each fell for a third straight session. Reuters said United States chipmakers dropped 5.3 percent, and that MSCI’s broadest index ended down 1.8 percent. Nvidia fell 4.1 percent and is down nearly 24 percent this month. Reuters tied that weakness in part to the departure last week of chief executive Jensen Huang. The company reports after the United States close today. Analysts, on that wire, expect earnings growth of 90 percent on revenue of $109 billion for Nvidia’s fiscal third quarter.

The dollar index, the Reuters basket of the greenback against major currencies, rose 0.19 percent to 103.60 and touched its highest level since April 2025. The euro was down 0.24 percent at $1.0995 in that New York close story. An earlier AWP Finanznachrichten dispatch, timestamped 21:12 in Zurich on Monday (15:12 in New York), had the euro at $1.1002 after an early-evening low of $1.0991 and an afternoon reading of $1.1012. Both prints are kept. Comments from Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh did not, on the Reuters account, dent expectations of a rate increase next month. Federal funds futures in that story imply a quarter-point hike at the December meeting, and the market is pricing at least three hikes between now and the end of 2027. The minutes of the October meeting are due on Wednesday.

The yield on the benchmark United States 10-year note fell 2.39 basis points to 5.129 percent, from 5.153 percent late on Friday, in the Reuters close story. Friday’s Tradeweb 3 p.m. close used in the prior briefing was 5.152 percent, one tenth of a basis point away from the Friday reference in this story. The 30-year yield fell 0.69 basis points to 5.4741 percent, from 5.481 percent.

Europe and Switzerland

Reuters, without naming the index in that sentence, said stocks in Europe ended down less than 0.3 percent after rising 2.5 percent to record highs last week. The named blue-chip closes were a bit weaker than that phrase. AWP, carried on finanzen.ch, put the EURO STOXX 50 at 6,348.35, down 0.60 percent. Dow Jones, via Morningstar, has the same close: down 38.51 points, or 0.60 percent, from Friday’s 6,386.86, and 3.10 percent under the 11 August record close of 6,551.22. The Financial Times Stock Exchange 100 closed at 10,892.93, down 0.09 percent, on the AWP Europe note. Dow Jones, in its 18:39 Zurich Europe file, put the DAX at 26,598.64, down 151.31 points, or 0.6 percent (the prose rounded the level to 26,599), and the CAC 40 at 8,352.00, down 16.91 points, or 0.2 percent.

Switzerland went the other way. AWP’s Swiss close put the Swiss Market Index up 0.57 percent at 15,192.58. The afternoon high was 15,272.42, described as a new all-time high, before a soft Wall Street open pulled the index back. The mid-cap SMIM fell 0.12 percent to 3,268.13. The broader Swiss Performance Index rose 0.42 percent to 21,346.00. Richemont gained 2.7 percent after Friday’s 3.8 percent drop on annual figures. Alcon and Novartis each rose 1.3 percent, Roche 1.1 percent, and Lonza 0.6 percent. AWP also had Novo Nordisk up 2.4 percent in the EURO STOXX 50 after study data for its next obesity drug, CagriSema. The same Europe note said the European technology sector index fell 1.3 percent.

The franc side of Monday evening, from AWP at 21:12 Zurich: the dollar at 0.8399 francs, after 0.8395 in the afternoon, and the euro at 0.9241 francs. Oil fell through the Monday session. Use the time on each print.

Three Brent readings, in order, and they are not the same price. CNBC, updated 6:29 a.m. Eastern on Monday, had Brent futures down 69 cents, or 0.7 percent, at $95.15, and West Texas Intermediate down 37 cents, or 0.4 percent, at $88.19. Dow Jones at 18:39 Zurich had Brent down 2.6 percent at $93.36 in the prose (the table printed 93.4) and West Texas Intermediate at $86.30, down 2.6 percent from Friday’s $88.56. The Reuters New York close story, the figure used as Monday’s settle in this briefing, had United States crude down 3.59 percent at $85.38 and Brent at $92.20, down 3.8 percent. Friday’s settles remain Brent $95.84 and West Texas Intermediate $88.56. The AWP Swiss-close line that Brent was “just under $94” was written before that New York settle.

CNBC attributed the surplus case to Friday’s International Energy Agency update: fourth-quarter supply averaging 105 million barrels a day, 1.7 million barrels a day above forecast demand, against a prior forecast of a fourth-quarter deficit. The agency also said 71 million barrels of a March emergency release would still be made available by the end of February, including 15 million barrels of diesel. The same CNBC piece said China’s National Bureau of Statistics showed October refinery throughput down 2 percent on the month and 8.1 percent on the year, and that a Reuters calculation from official data implied a Chinese crude inventory build of 492,000 barrels a day. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said two tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz and that six tankers caught fire on Sunday and Monday. Reuters could not independently verify that report. The recovery in export flows is the reason given for the lower settle; the unverified incidents are why the wires still call the surplus fragile.

Spot gold in the Reuters New York story fell 0.74 percent to $3,851.44 an ounce, after a session low of $3,830.74, the lowest since October 2025. Dow Jones’s earlier Europe table had gold at $3,867.69, down 0.3 percent from $3,880.04. Spot silver in the Reuters close fell 3.68 percent to $46.93. Copper fell 1.43 percent to $13,207.50 a ton.

Long-term exchange-traded fund lens

VWRA is the accumulating share class of the Vanguard FTSE All-World UCITS exchange-traded fund, ISIN IE00BK5BQT80, listed in Zurich in francs. It holds a physical sample of the FTSE All-World Index. The ongoing charge is 0.14 percent. For a holder who is not trading the week, Monday was a down day inside a wide range, not a break of the levels set for this note.

The franc market price and the dollar net asset value did not fall by the same amount. Vanguard shows a net asset value of $184.7690, down $3.21, or 1.71 percent, and a Zurich market price of 156.28 francs, down 0.88 francs, or 0.56 percent, both at the 16 November close. The dollar was firmer against the franc on the AWP evening print, which is the direction that cushions a franc holder when the dollar net asset value drops. That is not a full explanation of the gap: the fund can trade away from net asset value, and the two closes are not a calculated hedge ratio.

The published portfolio is still as at 31 October. The United States was 62.83 percent of the fund, Japan 5.94 percent, Switzerland 2.08 percent. Apple was 4.74363 percent, Nvidia 4.54553 percent, Microsoft 3.69160 percent. The fund held 3,574 stocks against 4,199 in the benchmark. The price/earnings ratio was 19.6 times. Monday’s chip decline is inside the fund, and it is not yet in that weight table. The Swiss Market Index making a record on a day Nasdaq fell 2.49 percent is the same point in index form: a Switzerland-listed world fund is not a Nasdaq position, even though American shares are most of it.

Nothing in Monday’s tape changes the reason to own the fund. It changes the week’s noise. The levels that matter for this briefing remain 140 and 135 francs. Both are intact.

Hottest tactical

Nvidia’s report after the United States close today, and Anthropic’s expected Nasdaq debut on Thursday. Reuters said Anthropic is expected to raise up to $80 billion, pricing on Wednesday, at a valuation near $1.5 trillion, and to begin trading on Thursday under the ticker ANT.O. Nvidia is already down nearly 24 percent this month and was the heaviest single name in a 5.3 percent drop for United States chipmakers. Yonhap, at mid-morning in Seoul, was still framing the session around those two events. For the world fund, Nvidia was 4.55 percent of assets at 31 October. That is large enough to move a day, and too small to be the fund.

Biggest risk

The risk to a long-term holder is treating this week’s artificial-intelligence test as a reason to leave a global fund, while the rate path that actually reprices the whole index is still pointed higher. Reuters said Warsh’s comments did not soften a market that is priced for a quarter-point Federal Reserve hike in December and at least three hikes by the end of 2027, with the 10-year yield at 5.129 percent. Oil can still flip that story: Monday’s settle fell because exports through the Strait of Hormuz looked more ample, and the same reports say the shipping risk is not closed. Jefferies strategist Mohit Kumar’s line in the Reuters story — that a Middle East ceasefire and oil under $80 would remove the case for further hikes in 2027 — is his view, not a forecast printed here as fact.

Fact versus speculation

Printed

  • The New York, Zurich, and named European cash closes above, each with the wire that printed them.
  • VWRA at 156.28 francs and a net asset value of $184.7690. Alerts at 140 and 135 were not hit.
  • The Nikkei close used here is the Kyodo 15:01 flash. Nikkei Inc.’s own file had not posted it yet.
  • Huang’s departure last week, as reported by Reuters, Dow Jones and Yonhap. Nvidia down 4.1 percent on Monday and nearly 24 percent this month, on Reuters.
  • Yonhap reported that the United States government on Monday issued rules restricting remote access to advanced American computing chips and servers. This briefing did not read the rule text.
  • The International Energy Agency figures cited above are Friday’s agency update as reported by CNBC, not a new Tuesday release.
  • Market pricing, which is a fact about prices and not a promise of what the Federal Reserve will do: a quarter-point December hike, and at least three hikes by the end of 2027, on Reuters.

Not established

  • Anthropic’s $80 billion raise, the near-$1.5 trillion valuation, Wednesday’s pricing and Thursday’s ANT.O listing are what Reuters said is expected. They have not happened.
  • Dow Jones said there was talk of a sixfold oversubscription. That is talk in that note.
  • Reports of a possible return by Huang, which Dow Jones said did not help the shares. A return is not a fact in the wires read this morning.
  • Any personal allegation around that departure. It is not re-reported here. A spying claim discussed in earlier briefings remains an allegation.
  • The Revolutionary Guards’ account of mined tankers. Reuters could not independently verify it.
  • Deutsche Bank’s forecast, in the Dow Jones Europe file, of three quarter-point hikes in December, January and March. Goldman Sachs’ Sunday note, via CNBC, that Brent averages $88 in 2027 and $78 by the end of 2027. Kumar’s ceasefire-and-sub-$80 condition. All three are forecasts.
  • The Shenzhen trilateral on Thursday. It is a 13 November New York Post report carried by Reuters, plus AWP’s later line that such a meeting is due this week. No White House confirmation was in the story when it was filed, and none was re-read this morning.
  • A closing level for the Korea Composite Stock Price Index, the Hang Seng, Shanghai, or the S&P/ASX 200. None is invented to fill the hour.
