Americas

CNBC’s note updated at 6:46 p.m. Eastern has the same three closes. The Campos page still shows a byline of 20 November, 1:46 a.m. UTC, marked updated. That clock is not the close. The index levels in the body match the 22:58 file, so the cross-asset figures in that wrap are used below as the post-close set.

CNBC said the Standard & Poor’s 500 and the Dow had both moved 10% below their closing records and entered correction territory, and that the Nasdaq Composite was down 2.7% for 2026, erasing its gain for the year. It also said the Nasdaq-100 fell 5.6% and traded in bear-market territory during the session, 20% under its recent high. That bear-market condition was described as intraday.

The Reuters close file put the Standard & Poor’s 500 and the Dow’s largest weekly drop since April 2025, and the Nasdaq’s largest losses since March 2020. The two wires agree on the Standard & Poor’s comparison and disagree on whether the Dow’s week was the worst since March 2020 or since April 2025. Both lines are left as printed.

Campos: MSCI’s all-country world gauge fell 34.89 points, or 3.30%, to 1,021.91, and its 8.1% weekly decline was the largest since early 2020. Those euro prints are not averaged.

The University of Michigan’s Friday survey, as reported by CNBC, put the headline sentiment index at 51.5, up 7.3% from a month earlier and in line with the Dow Jones consensus CNBC cited. One-year inflation expectations fell to 3.7%, a full percentage point below a month earlier and under the mid-month November reading of 4.3%, the lowest since March. The five-year expectation slipped to 3.3%.

CNBC, quoting Governor Lisa Cook in Chile: inflation has been above target for more than five and a half years, and waiting longer “risks getting behind the curve.” CNBC said markets strongly expect a quarter-point increase at the 8–9 December meeting. No fresh CME FedWatch percentage was in the Reuters or CNBC copies read this morning. AWP said the trade is counting on an increase of at least 25 basis points, and that some economists expect 50. That 50-basis-point line is expectation, not a priced probability.

Long-term ETF lens

VWRA is the accumulating share class of the Vanguard FTSE All-World UCITS (Undertakings for Collective Investment in Transferable Securities) exchange-traded fund, ISIN IE00BK5BQT80, priced in Swiss francs on SIX Swiss Exchange. It owns shares, it does not use a swap, and the ongoing charges figure is 0.14%. Vanguard’s page this morning showed 3,574 stocks against 4,199 in the FTSE All-World Index, a price/earnings ratio of 19.6 times, and a one-year tracking error of 0.07%, all as at 31 October. Share-class assets were $62.19 billion and total assets $88.84 billion.

The portfolio that matters for this account is not the Swiss Market Index. As at 31 October the fund was 62.83% United States, 5.94% Japan, 3.31% Taiwan, 2.25% South Korea and 2.08% Switzerland. The top holdings were Apple 4.74363%, Nvidia 4.54553% and Microsoft 3.69160%. Friday’s prices are not in that table. A 13.8% drop in Nvidia, a 5% drop in Seoul and Tokyo, and a 17% weekly figure for Taiwan — whichever of the two weekly clocks one uses — land in sleeves that were already a large part of the fund a month ago. Nestlé and Novartis rising in Zurich is the home market, not the fund.

From the two rows on Vanguard’s own history table, Friday 13 November to Friday 20 November, the franc market price went from 157.16 to 146.88, a decline of 10.28 francs, or 6.54%, and the net asset value went from $187.9772 to $172.9725, a decline of 7.98%. Those are arithmetic from the published rows, not a weekly statistic Vanguard printed. One week of that size does not change the reason to hold a single global equity fund. It does change the distance to the alerts the holder already set.

The Swiss close and the fund’s net asset value are different clocks. SIX stops at 17:30 in Zurich, which is 11:30 in New York. Vanguard stamps both the franc price and the dollar net asset value “at closure 20 Nov 2026.” The franc price fell 3.76% and the net asset value 3.27%. Part of any gap is the firmer franc — fewer francs per dollar — and part is that Zurich does not trade the last hours of Wall Street. This note does not try to split the two.

Hottest tactical tape

The tape traders were fighting on Friday was the round-trip in artificial intelligence, not a new macro print. Anthropic, ticker ANT, debuted on Thursday. Reuters (Campos) said that on Friday the shares fell as much as 14.5%, to just above the $83 initial-public-offering price, and ended down 9.1% at $88.30. CNBC’s story updated at 6:46 p.m. Eastern said Anthropic lost 8.5% and did not print a price. Agence France-Presse, in a Paris close story published at 18:38 — 12:38 in New York, so before the US cash close — said the shares were down 9.80% on the session and did not print a price. The only explicit price attached to the word “ended” is Reuters’ $88.30. The percentages are not averaged, and the AFP figure is not a New York close.

Around that print: Nvidia −13.8% (Reuters close file); the semiconductor index −10.7% on the day; Seoul and Tokyo each down more than 5%; consumer staples the only green Standard & Poor’s sector, with CNBC citing gains of more than 1% for Walmart and about 1% for Costco, Johnson & Johnson and Procter & Gamble. Reuters reported, citing two people briefed on the matter, that Securities and Exchange Commission enforcement staff are looking at possible accounting issues at Nvidia. The agency declined to comment. Nvidia did not immediately respond. Reuters could not establish when the inquiry opened or which issues are in scope, and wrote that an investigation is not evidence of wrongdoing and that many close with no action. At the time of that story — the page shows 7:13 p.m. UTC, which is 2:13 p.m. in New York, not the close — the shares were down about 13% after having been down more than 6% before publication.

The same Reuters account calls Jensen Huang the ousted chief executive and Colette Kress the interim chief executive and former chief financial officer, speaking in that role on Tuesday’s earnings call. It says Huang admitted an affair in a statement on 8 November, and that China has denied Alice Huang is a spy. Those personal claims are allegations in a news story, not findings of this note. They mattered for the tape because Reuters tied them to the selling.

For this account the tactical fact is narrower. VWRA already owns the basket. Trading the day’s most violent names would be a second decision, on top of a fund that has already marked the move in its net asset value. Nothing in Friday’s closes requires a sale to stay with the stated alerts.

Biggest risk

The risk that reaches this fund is a Federal Reserve that is still expected to tighten, meeting an equity drawdown that Friday did not finish. The next policy meeting is 8–9 December. CNBC said a quarter-point increase is strongly expected. A half-point is what AWP said some economists look for, and what CNBC’s week-ahead piece treated as the fear that Wednesday’s inflation report could cool. October personal consumption expenditures, the Fed’s preferred price gauge, are due Wednesday 25 November at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, in a holiday-shortened week. Cook’s warning, as carried by CNBC, is that waiting could mean larger increases later.

The second risk is oil and the war, because it feeds that same inflation path. Friday’s settlement rose on renewed attack worries and pre-holiday buying, even though the week was still down and Hormuz flows were described as near pre-war levels. A mine claim carried by Iranian state media, and a Houthi threat to Saudi ports, are in the Reuters settlement story as statements, not as damage Reuters counted itself. Either one becoming a sustained interruption would sit on top of a portfolio that is equities only.
