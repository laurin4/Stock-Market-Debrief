Americas

United States stocks ended a volatile Thursday with modest losses. Nvidia finished 4.3 percent lower, well off a drop that Bloomberg later described as more than 14 percent at the worst of the session. The company said chief financial officer Colette Kress will serve as interim chief executive while it looks for a permanent successor. A semiconductor index fell 0.5 percent. Advanced Micro Devices rose 5.9 percent and Broadcom rose 5.5 percent. Alibaba fell 7.1 percent in United States trading. MSCI’s gauge of stocks across the globe fell 0.44 percent to 1,116.27.

The dollar index reached a 19-month high. In the equity wrap it rose 0.3 percent to 103.18. In the dollar wrap it was last up 0.29 percent at 103.17. The dollar was last up 0.26 percent at 0.835 Swiss francs, a one-month high. One franc at that print is the reciprocal, about 1.198 dollars. That reciprocal is arithmetic, not a second market quote.

Rate markets did the real work. The 2-year Treasury yield, which both Reuters wraps agree on, rose 13.96 basis points to 4.867 percent, the highest since 14 October. CME FedWatch, as cited by Reuters’ dollar story, priced a near 85 percent chance of a December hike by the Federal Reserve. The equity wrap said fed-funds futures priced 29 basis points of December tightening, which it read as slim odds of a 50-basis-point move. A separate Friday Reuters note, from the Asian morning, put the chance of that 50-basis-point move at 14.5 percent, up from zero a week earlier. Those three readings are quoted as published. They are not combined into a new probability.

The reason the hike is priced at all is Tuesday’s Consumer Price Index. The Bureau of Labor Statistics said the index rose 0.4 percent in October, against a Reuters poll of 0.3 percent, after 0.6 percent in September. The core index, excluding food and energy, rose 0.3 percent for a third straight month. The Bureau did not publish a year-over-year rate, because it did not collect October 2025 prices during a lapse in appropriations. October producer prices are due today at 14:30 Zurich (08:30 Eastern Time), after this briefing.

Europe and Switzerland

Europe held up better than New York. The STOXX Europe 600 closed 0.14 percent higher at 664.01 after touching a record 666.58, Reuters reported. Germany’s DAX added 0.4 percent and also set a record. Reuters did not print the DAX level in that story, so none is given here. The Euro Stoxx 50 closed up 0.09 percent at 6,398.98, according to AWP. The Swiss Market Index gained 0.63 percent to 15,149.43. AWP said the FTSE 100 finished firmer and did not give the level.

European technology fell 0.5 percent. The damage stayed there. Siemens rose 1.7 percent after raising its medium-term growth outlook. Adecco jumped 14.7 percent on a stronger-than-expected third-quarter operating profit. Zurich Insurance gained 2 percent; AWP said analysts were, on balance, positive on the nine-month figures. Reuters noted that the STOXX 600 is up nearly 5 percent this quarter while the Standard & Poor’s 500 is down 0.2 percent, and that luxury stocks and autos are each down more than 17 percent this year.

Into Friday, Euro Stoxx 50 futures were down 0.4 percent as of 05:54 London time, Bloomberg reported. Germany’s 10-year yield was up 3 basis points at 3.35 percent, and Britain’s 10-year yield was up 8 basis points at 5.30 percent, in that same snapshot. Cash Europe and the Swiss exchange open after this note.

Long-term ETF lens

VWRA is the accumulating share class of Vanguard’s FTSE All-World UCITS ETF, ISIN IE00BK5BQT80, listed in francs on SIX. It does not distribute income. Vanguard’s ongoing charges figure / total expense ratio is 0.14 percent. The fund physically samples the FTSE All-World Index. As of 30 September 2026 it held 3,821 stocks against 4,203 in the index, with a price/earnings ratio of 20.7 times, the same as the benchmark. Those portfolio figures are six weeks old.

Country weights on that date: United States 62.24 percent, Japan 6.06 percent, Taiwan 3.54 percent, United Kingdom 3.12 percent, Switzerland 1.88 percent. The largest holding was Nvidia at 5.01 percent of fund market value, then Apple 4.52 percent and Microsoft 3.58 percent. A leadership break at a 5 percent holding matters, and it is still one holding inside a fund that owns several thousand companies. Share-class assets were $62.19 billion on the page read this morning.

Thursday’s dollar net asset value was $188.6596, down $0.79 or 0.42 percent. The franc market price rose 0.16 percent on the same day. That is what an unhedged global fund does when the dollar firms against the franc: the basket got cheaper in dollars, and the franc weakened enough (dollar at 0.835) that the SIX price still edged up. Over a long holding period the franc price will follow world equities and the franc’s exchange rate together. A higher Federal Reserve path supports the dollar, which cushions the franc quote on a down day for equities, and it also raises the discount rate on a 20.7 times portfolio. Both effects are in the same fund. Neither is a reason, by itself, to trade around a long-term holding.

The 52-week market-value high on Vanguard’s page is 162.38 francs and the low is 129.36 francs, both labeled as of 13 November. Vanguard states the 52-week difference as 33.02 francs, or +20.34 percent. Thursday’s close is 4.20 francs under that high, about 2.6 percent, using the high as the base. It is 28.82 francs above that low, about 22.3 percent, using the low as the base. Those two comparisons are arithmetic on Vanguard’s printed high and low. They are not a restatement of Vanguard’s +20.34 percent figure.

Hottest tactical

The tape’s hot trade is a rotation, not a new destination. Nvidia’s loss was partly offset, on the day, by Advanced Micro Devices and Broadcom. Brent’s settle, up $3.06, was the day’s inflation impulse, while AI-linked names stayed in question. Europe, with less of the index in that trade, made a record intraday high and still closed up. For a fund that already owns the winners, the losers, the oil majors and the Swiss insurers at index weight, the tactical version of this story is a bet that the rotation continues for days or weeks. That is a trading view. It is not required to keep a long-term VWRA holding intact. Chasing Thursday’s relative winners would add a second decision on top of a fund that rebalances itself toward whatever the index does next.

Biggest risk

The risk that reaches the whole fund is the discount rate, not the single-stock headline. A 10-year yield back above 5 percent, a December hike that traders already treat as likely, a live tail of a 50-basis-point move, and Brent settling at $95.82 all point the same way. Today’s producer-price index is the next official test. Bloomberg quoted Pepperstone’s Dilin Wu: a meaningful downside surprise would be needed to challenge December-hike pricing, and another hot print could push rate expectations higher and pressure equities. New York Fed president John Williams said Thursday, per Bloomberg, that inflation remains high and that policymakers have to take further action. Capital Group’s Manusha Samaraweera told Reuters that three hikes are priced over roughly the next 12 months, and that further hot prints could make that path real. Those are cited views, not a forecast of this briefing.

The second risk is narrower and already inside the top holding. JPMorgan analysts wrote that Huang’s departure leaves a vacuum, that they do not assume existing commitments change, and that new deals could take longer. Nvidia’s third-quarter results are due Tuesday, Bloomberg reported. A bad combination for a long-term holder would be a hotter producer-price index today and a messy Nvidia report next week, because one hits the multiple and the other hits the largest position. A good combination would be the opposite. Neither outcome is known this morning.

Fact versus speculation

Reported as fact

  • Index closes, yields, currencies and commodity settles printed above, each tied to the dispatch named in Sources. Where two Reuters lasts differ, both are printed.
  • Nvidia said Jensen Huang has resigned and that Colette Kress is interim chief executive. The shares closed down 4.3 percent.
  • October Consumer Price Index rose 0.4 percent on the month; core rose 0.3 percent. No official year-over-year rate was published.
  • VWRA’s franc close, dollar net asset value, charges, holdings and 52-week marks are Vanguard’s figures, with the holdings date stated.
  • Producer prices are on the Bureau of Labor Statistics calendar for 14:30 Zurich today. They were not out when this page was written.
  • The Federal Reserve Bank of New York said it bought 79.4 billion yen ($497.37 million) in the spot market on 31 July, selling euros from the Exchange Stabilization Fund, as part of joint intervention this summer.

Allegation, claim, or forecast

  • Reuters reports that the resignation follows news of an affair with a Chinese businesswoman whom United States authorities suspect of spying for Beijing. The suspicion is an allegation in that reporting. This briefing does not treat it as established.
  • Iran’s military said four oil tankers were hit by explosions in the Strait of Hormuz. Reuters said it could not immediately verify those accounts. Yemen’s Houthis said they struck Saudi energy sites. Those are claims alongside a verified price move.
  • Reuters’ description of fighting since February as a US-Israeli war with Iran is the wire’s framing of the backdrop for oil and inflation. It is attributed, not re-reported from a battlefield source.
  • Any path for a 50-basis-point hike, for three hikes in a year, for intervention near 160 yen, or for Nvidia’s export licences and Tuesday’s earnings, is a market or analyst view.
  • Friday’s Asian index levels are intraday. The early Reuters bounce and the later Bloomberg fade are both real dispatches. Neither is an official close, and they are not blended.
