Americas

United States cash equities closed sharply lower on Thursday after Anthropic, the company behind the Claude assistant, opened on Nasdaq. Reuters’ closing wrap (Stephen Culp and Ragini Mathur) and the later global wrap (Rodrigo Campos, Euronext page stamped 23:00) print the same index levels: the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1,101.11 points, or 2.19 percent, to 49,097.06; the Standard & Poor’s 500 lost 147.80 points, or 2.02 percent, to 7,175.83; the Nasdaq Composite lost 550.68 points, or 2.25 percent, to 23,879.20. CNBC said that was the Dow’s first close below 50,000 since June. Culp said the S&P 500 logged a sixth straight loss and was on course for its steepest weekly decline since April 2025. The week is not over: Friday’s United States cash session had not started at this hour.

Campos’s global file put MSCI’s all-country gauge at 1,056.80, down 17.76 points or 1.65 percent, and emerging-market stocks at 1,465.49, down 1.27 percent. On the New York Stock Exchange, declining issues beat advancers by 4.37 to 1. Volume was 20.83 billion shares, against a 19.00 billion average over the prior 20 full sessions (Culp).

Anthropic was priced at $83. Culp: the shares opened at $111.50, traded as high as $115 (38.6 percent above the offer), and the valuation briefly went above $2 trillion before the gain was cut to 17.1 percent. The Associated Press, in a story timestamped 10:17 p.m. Eastern on Thursday, said the stock finished at $97.17, still up 17.1 percent. Campos rounded the close to “up 17 percent” and a valuation just under $1.8 trillion, and put the high at $115.10, “as much as 39 percent.” Those are different prints of the same session, not a range to average. The Europe close file said the company raised $81.3 billion. The Swiss wire AWP described total placement proceeds of $107 billion. Both figures are left as each wire printed them.

Nvidia fell 3.3 percent, a sixth straight loss (Culp). The Associated Press reported the resignation of Jensen Huang as chief executive. A Reuters Morning Bid dated 18 November said the board will not rehire him after that departure. Advanced Micro Devices fell 7.7 percent after Hunterbrook said its affiliated fund was short the shares (Culp). Micron and Broadcom each fell 2.1 percent. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index closed 2.4 percent lower, and the broader technology sector ended down 1.9 percent. CoreWeave fell 6.1 percent and Nebius 9.4 percent. Palo Alto Networks rose 6.4 percent after it raised its 2027 revenue guidance. Walmart slipped 1.0 percent after its annual sales forecast.

Culp, citing CME Group’s FedWatch tool: a 96.4 percent likelihood of a 25 basis-point increase at the December meeting, and a small chance of 50 basis points. That is a snapshot inside one story, not a later quote. The same story said analysts expect next week’s core personal consumption expenditures price index to be up 3.2 percent on the year. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said on Thursday that the evidence did not suggest inflation was heading back to 2 percent on its own, and that the rollout of artificial intelligence could add to inflation the Fed would have to counteract (Reuters, Li Gu and Rae Wee, Friday).

Europe and Switzerland

The STOXX Europe 600 closed down 0.88 percent at 638.41, a fourth straight decline and the lowest close since 23 October (Reuters, Anjana Jayakrishnan Nair and Avinash P, Euronext stamp 19:33). Food and beverage fell 2.1 percent and healthcare 1.8 percent. ASML rose 2.2 percent and STMicroelectronics 2 percent; both, and Infineon, had given back part of larger early gains by the close. Novartis fell 3.2 percent after it set mid-term sales-growth targets at the lower end of its previous range. Sanofi fell 2.9 percent after Bloomberg News reported it was weighing a purchase of Insmed.

Asia and commodities

Thursday’s official Tokyo close, from the Nikkei Indexes daily file, is still the last row on that file: Nikkei 225 at 59,444.21, from an open of 59,025.19, a high of 59,660.61 and a low of 58,750.53. The file’s update line still read 19 November when it was opened this morning.

Friday’s session was a different day. The Nikkei newspaper’s morning-close article (11:51 Japan time) put the morning finish at 56,775.04, down 2,669.17 points or 4.49 percent. The loss exceeded 2,800 points during the morning, the lowest intraday level since April. Topix’s morning close was 3,703.30, down 119.17 points or 3.12 percent. SoftBank Group was down more than 16 percent at one point and traded under 3,000 yen. The Associated Press, describing morning trading, matches that Nikkei level: 56,775.04, down 4.5 percent.

Nikkei’s own market page, stamped as updated at 15:00 Japan time, showed 56,436.65, down 3,007.56 points from Thursday’s official close (5.06 percent, calculated from those two Nikkei figures, not a percentage printed on the page). The same screen ranked SoftBank Group down 14.67 percent, which is a smaller loss than the morning extreme. Treat 56,436.65 as the exchange screen at the closing bell. It is not yet a row in the Nikkei Indexes daily history.

South Korea had not produced a Yonhap closing flash in the pages read. The Associated Press’s early print was 4,905.20, down 4.5 percent. The Asia Business Daily, at 13:33 Korea time, had the Kospi at 4,873.87, down 264.02 points or 5.14 percent, with a sell-side program-trading halt after Kospi 200 futures fell 5.04 percent at 11:54. Samsung Electronics and SK hynix were each down 6.5 percent in that report. Seoul’s cash close is 15:30 Korea time, which is 07:30 in Zurich, so those figures are not the close.

Hong Kong and Shanghai were still open at 07:30 Zurich. The Associated Press early-Friday dispatch, not a close, had the Hang Seng nearly 2 percent lower at 22,393.93 and the Shanghai Composite down 1.7 percent at 3,515.87. Australia’s S&P/ASX 200 was down 1.6 percent at 8,594.10 in that same early dispatch; Sydney’s close was not in the pages read.

Oil settlements, Reuters (Siddharth Cavale, Euronext stamp 21:42): Brent settled up 3 cents, or 0.03 percent, at $91.98. West Texas Intermediate settled down 5 cents, or 0.06 percent, at $83.21, the lowest settlement since 26 August. Iran’s navy, via state media, said two tankers hit naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz and exploded. Reuters could not independently verify that report. Saudi civil defence, via the state news agency, said missiles fired by the Houthis damaged several floors of the sovereign-wealth-fund headquarters in Riyadh and nearby buildings, with no injuries reported. The International Energy Agency said cumulative losses from the Middle East conflict have reached 2.8 billion barrels of oil and 60 billion cubic metres of gas, and that the conflict could cut world oil demand by more than 4 percent by 2035 if governments accelerate electrification and fuel switching. That 2035 line is the agency’s scenario, not a forecast used here as fact.

The same Reuters oil story said Presidents Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin agreed a symbolic “peace declaration” on Thursday, repeated that a nuclear war should never be fought, and pledged to increase fuel supplies. Trump said he and Xi felt strongly that oil has to flow through Hormuz, and that the United States would “see what happens” in the conflict with Iran after 1 January, without explaining the date. Mandia’s Europe copy placed the meeting on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific summit in Shenzhen.

Already out this morning: China’s loan prime rate was left unchanged, the one-year rate at 3.0 percent and the five-year-and-longer rate at 3.5 percent (Xinhua, 10:03 Beijing time). Reuters called it an eighteenth straight month. Xinhua said the weighted average rate on new corporate loans in October was about 2.9 percent, and on new mortgages about 3.1 percent. Japan’s core consumer price index rose 2.2 percent in October, a second straight month above the Bank of Japan’s 2 percent target (Reuters, Li and Wee). The story did not print a headline rate beside that core figure.

A sixth down day in the S&P 500, and a violent Friday morning in Tokyo, sit inside a fund that already owns those markets. Anthropic is not in the 31 October holdings list. Nvidia is. Nothing on this page is a signal to sell a long-term holding, and nothing here says the next close will be higher. The levels worth keeping are the two franc alerts below, which were not touched.

Hottest tactical

The tape’s focus is the Anthropic debut, not a Swiss defensive stock. The offer was $83, the open was $111.50, the high was $115 in Culp and $115.10 in Campos, and the Associated Press close was $97.17, up 17.1 percent. Traders told AWP and the Nikkei that other artificial-intelligence shares were being sold to fund that purchase. That is market talk. Culp’s portfolio-manager quote says the opposite caution: one day’s trading, up or down, should not be read as the verdict on the whole theme, because buyers have to fund the new shares from somewhere. For a holder of the whole world index, the listing matters later only if and when it enters the index. Today’s holdings file cannot say when that would be.

Biggest risk

The risk that actually shows up in the prices is tighter financial conditions on a fund that is still about 63 percent American shares. The dollar index has risen for eight sessions and sits at a 19-month high, the S&P 500 has fallen for six, and the FedWatch snapshot in the Reuters equity close put a December quarter-point increase at 96.4 percent, with a smaller chance of a half-point. Goolsbee added that artificial-intelligence investment itself may push inflation up. That combination hits the same mega-cap technology book VWRA already owns, including a Nvidia position whose chief executive has left and whose board, Reuters reported, will not bring him back.

The Hormuz mine claim is a separate headline. Reuters could not verify it, and Brent settled 3 cents higher. It is not the risk the oil settlement priced. Treat it as unverified until a second source confirms the tankers.
