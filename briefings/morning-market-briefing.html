Morning Market Briefing

Thursday 12 November 2026 · prepared for 07:30 Central European Time · cash figures are Wednesday 11 November closes unless marked as a Thursday morning indication

VWRA is the Swiss-listed accumulating share class of the Vanguard FTSE All-World UCITS ETF (undertakings for collective investment in transferable securities). Dollar/franc is Wednesday’s Reuters currencies report. Brent is a Thursday morning indication, after a Wednesday settlement of $92.76.

Americas

The regular session was a broad rebound. Reuters reported the Dow Jones Industrial Average up 254.60 points, or 0.49 percent, to 52,486.49; the Standard & Poor’s 500 (S&P 500) up 98.45 points, or 1.31 percent, to 7,619.38; and the Nasdaq Composite up 400.31 points, or 1.60 percent, to 25,466.19. MSCI’s gauge of stocks across the globe rose 1.12 percent to 1,121.15. The S&P 500 technology sector gained 2.7 percent. In Reuters’ Trading Day column, semiconductors rose 5.6 percent and energy 1.8 percent, and consumer discretionary was the only S&P 500 sector lower.

Nvidia gained 2.9 percent in the regular session, to 173.73 dollars on the price table published with that column, recovering part of a sharp prior loss. After the close it fell 8 percent. Reuters, citing the Wall Street Journal and people familiar with the matter, reported that the board was preparing to oust chief executive Jensen Huang after two extramarital affairs, including one with a woman suspected of spying for China. CoreWeave finished up 24.7 percent, at 60.77 dollars. Reuters said the company expects to become profitable in 2028; its Europe close story specified adjusted profitability in the first half of 2028. The same price table showed Micron up 11.89 percent to 811.30 dollars and Dell up 11.75 percent to 373.68. Microsoft fell 2.26 percent to 521.13.

The move sat on top of Tuesday’s inflation report and last week’s United States midterm elections, which Reuters said left Democrats in control of Congress and had already knocked technology shares on concern about artificial-intelligence regulation. The Treasury market was shut on Wednesday for Veterans Day, a day after two-year yields had their biggest one-day jump since April 2025. Euro-area yields steadied.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics said the Consumer Price Index (CPI) rose 0.4 percent in October, against a 0.3 percent gain expected in a Reuters poll, after 0.6 percent in September. It was the third straight month of a rise of 0.4 percent or more. Core CPI, excluding food and energy, rose 0.3 percent for a third month; economists had expected 0.2 percent. The agency published no year-over-year rate, because last year’s federal shutdown left no October 2025 comparison. Economists’ reconstructions cited by Reuters put the missing annual CPI around 3.9 to 4.0 percent, versus 3.7 percent in September. Shelter, including rents and hotel rooms, accounted for more than 30 percent of the monthly increase. Food rose 0.5 percent. Gasoline rose 1.0 percent after 9.1 percent in September.

The Federal Reserve left the benchmark overnight rate in a 3.75 to 4.00 percent range at its October meeting. Governor Christopher Waller said on Tuesday the central bank should raise rates next month and that “a stronger policy move may be needed,” including the prospect of 50 basis points (0.50 percentage point). By Wednesday’s equity close, Reuters said traders, using the CME FedWatch tool, treated a hike next month as a certainty, with roughly an 11 percent chance of a half-point move. A separate Reuters currencies report put the chance of a 25-basis-point December hike at 87 percent, up from about 70 percent a month earlier. Goldman Sachs, Reuters reported, now looks for hikes in December and March. Deutsche Bank looks for December, January and March.

The dollar index, against a basket including the yen and the euro, rose 0.7 percent to 102.92 in the equity-close story, with the euro down 0.68 percent at 1.1087 dollars and the dollar up 0.69 percent at 159.83 yen. The currencies copy, taken at a slightly different moment, had the index up 0.63 percent at 102.85, its highest since April 2025, and the dollar up 0.86 percent at 0.832 Swiss francs, the strongest since mid-October. Reuters’ Trading Day column called it a 19-month high for the dollar and a 19-month low for the euro, and listed the Swiss franc among the day’s weakest Group of Ten currencies. Gold, in that same column, was down about 1 percent and back below 4,000 dollars an ounce. A finer spot price was not confirmed in a full gold story, so none is printed here.

Europe and Switzerland

The STOXX Europe 600 gained 0.72 percent to 663.05, a record close, and finished just short of the day’s intraday high of 663.41, Reuters reported. Germany’s DAX rose 1.2 percent. The euro-zone benchmark, the Euro STOXX 50, rose 1.04 percent to 6,393.39, its highest in two months, and stopped short of 6,400, dpa-AFX reported. The FTSE 100 in London gained 0.8 percent. It was Europe’s third straight up day on the euro-zone index.

Siemens Energy jumped 10.8 percent after raising its 2027 profit-margin expectations and announcing plans to more than triple its dividend. Schneider Electric rose 3 percent and Legrand 2.2 percent. The European technology sector added 2.5 percent. Soitec rose 11.9 percent and Aixtron 8 percent. Czech arms maker CSG fell 21.6 percent after cutting its full-year operating-margin forecast, and that weighed on defence shares. Retail and food and beverage lagged. Societe Generale analysts wrote that the rally looks stretched and that, with the STOXX 600 already near their upside-risk target, they see limited further upside by year-end. Traders, Reuters said, expect at least one quarter-point hike from the European Central Bank (ECB) by February 2027.

Switzerland lagged the artificial-intelligence bounce. The Swiss Market Index (SMI) gained 0.11 percent to 15,053.86, dpa-AFX reported. Dow Jones, rounding the same session, had it up 0.1 percent at 15,054. Twelve of the twenty SMI names rose and eight fell. Turnover was 24.31 million shares, against 20.36 million on Tuesday. Dow Jones noted that, apart from Logitech, the SMI holds no true technology stock and is light on cyclical names, so it did not participate fully in the regional rebound.

Logitech led the SMI, up 4.0 percent. ABB rose 2.5 percent, which traders linked to Siemens Energy’s results. Alcon fell 3.9 percent: third-quarter revenue growth missed analysts’ expectations, and the company narrowed full-year profit guidance to the lower end of the previous range. Vontobel said implant sales disappointed because of competitive pressure in the United States. Swiss Life fell 1.5 percent. Premium income was higher over nine months, while net new money and direct investment income were weaker. Roche, Nestlé and Novartis fell by as much as 1.3 percent. Outside the SMI, SoftwareOne fell 4.3 percent even after a higher margin outlook, because third-quarter revenue growth slowed relative to the nine-month pace. Ypsomed rose 4.9 percent after results and a higher annual forecast.

European and Swiss cash markets were still shut at 07:30 Zurich time. The SIX Swiss Exchange opens at 09:00.

Asia and commodities

Wednesday’s Asian session had already been firm: Reuters’ Trading Day column recorded Japan up 1.3 percent and China up 0.4 percent. Thursday’s session, still in progress at briefing time, was mixed and nervous around Nvidia. Agence France-Presse, at the Tokyo break, had the Nikkei 225 up 0.8 percent at 61,714.16, the Kospi in Seoul up 1.2 percent at 5,463.98, the Shanghai Composite up 0.4 percent at 3,694.28, and the Hang Seng in Hong Kong down 1.1 percent at 24,043.69. AFP said chipmakers including SK hynix, Samsung, Kioxia and Taiwan Semiconductor had led the regional bounce, and that Nvidia shares were down around 20 percent since the affair was reported at the end of last month. These are morning indications, not official closes.

On the same AFP snapshot the euro was at 1.1097 dollars, from 1.1092, and the dollar was at 159.60 yen, from 159.86. That is a later, slightly softer dollar reading than Wednesday’s New York close.

Oil reversed the prior day’s gain. Brent settled Wednesday up 1.27 dollars, or 1.39 percent, at 92.76 dollars a barrel, and West Texas Intermediate settled up 49 cents, or 0.57 percent, at 86.53, Reuters reported, after Iran’s security chief said Tehran would not allow enriched nuclear material to leave the country. By 04:26 GMT on Thursday, Brent was down 1.47 dollars, or 1.58 percent, at 91.29, and West Texas Intermediate was down 1.24 dollars, or 1.43 percent, at 85.29. Reuters attributed the drop to easier concern about a wider Middle East escalation, higher OPEC+ output (the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries and allies), and a stock build. Market sources citing the American Petroleum Institute said United States crude stocks rose by 9 million barrels. The Energy Information Administration (EIA) report is due Thursday, delayed a day by Wednesday’s holiday.

Iran’s security chief, Mohsen Rezaei, said the country will not allow even a particle of enriched material to leave and that there would be consequences if the United States launched new strikes. Washington has said nuclear issues must be part of any first talks aimed at ending what Reuters described as an eight-month-old war, and United States officials played down the need for further strikes. In Abu Dhabi, Russian and Ukrainian negotiators agreed a large prisoner exchange; United States mediators said differences remained, including on territory. A strategist quoted by Reuters tied the oil drop to ceasefire hopes and to a view that the Iran war is unlikely to escalate ahead of a United States–China meeting next week. Shipping risk in the Strait of Hormuz is still the reason Reuters’ sources do not expect a collapse in the price. Wednesday’s European rally story had also linked the prior Brent rebound to shipping attacks in the strait.

Long-term ETF lens

VWRA seeks to track the FTSE All-World Index by buying a representative sample of the stocks in it. Vanguard lists an ongoing-charges figure of 0.14 percent, physical replication, accumulating dividends, Irish domicile, and 3,821 holdings against 4,203 in the benchmark, as of 30 September 2026. The fund’s price-to-earnings ratio was 20.7 times on that date. The United States was 62.24 percent of the fund. Switzerland was 1.88 percent. Nvidia was the largest holding, at 5.01 percent, ahead of Apple at 4.52 percent and Microsoft at 3.58 percent. Taiwan Semiconductor was 1.78 percent. Those weights are end-September, so they have drifted with prices since then.

For a saver who adds in Swiss francs over many years, Wednesday contained two different effects. The fund’s net asset value (NAV) in dollars rose 1.08 percent, to 189.45 dollars, on the 11 November valuation. The price on the SIX Swiss Exchange rose 2.20 percent, to 157.92 francs. The franc was one of the weakest major currencies on the day, so a dollar-based global fund translated into more francs than the underlying dollar NAV move alone. New francs committed today buy fewer units than they would have at Tuesday’s close. Units already held are worth more in francs.

The index question underneath the headlines is the discount rate. A December Federal Reserve hike is treated in the rates market as the base case, and a half-point move is an open possibility, while October prices rose faster than economists expected for a third month. Higher policy rates lean against the valuations of long-duration growth companies, which are a large part of this fund through its United States weight. Europe’s earnings picture, as framed in the Reuters column from Nuveen, is strong and narrow: with about half of STOXX 600 companies reported, third-quarter earnings were tracking around 24 percent year-on-year growth, and energy, artificial-intelligence-exposed technology and financials accounted for roughly 85 percent of that aggregate growth.

A single-company governance shock in a 5 percent holding changes the path of a global fund. It does not change the reason the fund exists, which is to own the large- and mid-sized stock markets of developed and emerging countries in one accumulating share class. The 140 and 135 franc alerts are levels to notice. Both sit well under Wednesday’s close, so neither is active this morning.

Hottest tactical

The tape’s hot trade is the two-day repair in artificial-intelligence infrastructure. CoreWeave’s path to adjusted profit in the first half of 2028, Siemens Energy’s higher 2027 margin aim and dividend plan, and a 5.6 percent rise in the semiconductor group pulled money back into the same complex that sold off after the midterms. In Switzerland the expression was narrow: Logitech and ABB, with the defensive heavyweights down.

The trade was already being marked down after the cash close. Nvidia’s 8 percent after-hours drop, on a report about the chief executive rather than on a new demand print, is the overhang for the United States session later today. Asia’s chip leaders were still higher at the Tokyo break. That split — cash markets up, the largest artificial-intelligence stock down after hours — is the tactical fact. It is a sentiment swing measured in hours, sitting on an unresolved board decision.

Biggest risk

The risk that can reprice both the discount rate and the fund’s largest holding at once is a firmer Federal Reserve landing in the middle of the Nvidia leadership crisis. Inflation has accelerated for three months, oil is still a geopolitical instrument, and the dollar is at its strongest since the spring of 2025. A 25-basis-point December hike is the market’s central case. A 50-basis-point hike is what Waller put in play, and what Wednesday’s FedWatch reading still assigned roughly an 11 percent probability. Either way, the October hold at 3.75 to 4.00 percent is what Bank of America’s Stephen Juneau, quoted by Reuters, called a policy that now looks behind the curve.

If the Nvidia board removes Huang, or if the reported national-security allegations tighten export rules, the shock travels through a holding that was 5 percent of VWRA at the last full portfolio date, and through the Taiwan and Korea supply chain around it. If the board keeps him and the investigation fades, Wednesday’s rebound can extend. Both outcomes are open. The oil price can push the inflation story either way inside a day: Brent settled up 1.4 percent on Wednesday and was down 1.6 percent by early Thursday, with Hormuz still the floor under the bearish case.

Fact versus speculation

Reported as fact

  • Wednesday cash closes and percentage changes for the S&P 500, Nasdaq, Dow, STOXX 600, Euro STOXX 50 and SMI, as cited above.
  • October CPI up 0.4 percent and core CPI up 0.3 percent. No official annual rate, because October 2025 prices were not collected.
  • Federal funds rate still 3.75 to 4.00 percent. Waller said a larger move may be needed. Wednesday’s Reuters close put a December hike as certain in FedWatch, with about an 11 percent chance of 50 basis points.
  • Nvidia up 2.9 percent in regular trading and down 8 percent after the bell. The after-hours figure is an extended-hours indication.
  • CoreWeave up 24.7 percent. The company said it expects to be profitable in 2028; Reuters’ Europe story said adjusted profitability in the first half of 2028.
  • Dollar/franc 0.832, up 0.86 percent. VWRA SIX close 157.92 francs, up 2.20 percent. Dollar NAV 189.45, up 1.08 percent.
  • Brent settlement 92.76 dollars. Thursday 04:26 GMT indication 91.29 dollars. American Petroleum Institute crude stocks up 9 million barrels, via market sources in the Reuters oil story.
  • Gold down about 1 percent and back below 4,000 dollars an ounce, per Reuters’ Trading Day market-moves list. No separate verified spot print.

Reported, still unresolved

  • Whether Nvidia’s board will remove Jensen Huang. Reuters reported that the Wall Street Journal said the board was preparing to do so. That is a report about intention. It is not a company announcement.
  • The espionage claim. AFP reported that Huang admitted a relationship with Alice Huang, a senior executive at the Singapore data-centre firm Megaspeed, and that a United States intelligence memo alleged she was an asset of China’s Ministry of State Security. AFP also reported a New York Times account of a second relationship, with Nvidia’s vice president of health care, Kimberly Powell. These are allegations as published. They are not a finding in this briefing.
  • AFP’s phrasing that Huang is “facing the boot,” and Saxo’s comment, quoted by AFP, that Nvidia has no publicly identified successor. Both are interpretation.
  • Economists’ 3.9 to 4.0 percent guess at the missing October annual CPI, and the 0.4 percent / 3.3 percent core PCE estimate. Both are reconstructions ahead of Friday’s PPI.
  • Goldman Sachs and Deutsche Bank hike calendars. Forecasts, not a Federal Reserve plan.
  • A strategist’s link between Thursday’s oil drop, ceasefire efforts, and a United States–China meeting next week. The meeting is his premise in the Reuters story, not a date confirmed in that article.
  • Societe Generale’s view that STOXX 600 upside is limited into year-end. An opinion.
