Americas

United States stocks finished Friday mixed, and the week still closed higher. Nothing in the Sunday re-read replaced those closes. The Standard & Poor’s 500 fell 30.73 points, or 0.40 percent, to 7,561.67. The Nasdaq Composite fell 229.82 points, or 0.91 percent, to 25,136.27. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 50.46 points, or 0.10 percent, to 52,347.55. For the week the Standard & Poor’s 500 gained 2.51 percent, the Nasdaq rose 2.27 percent and the Dow climbed 2.07 percent. Reuters said the Nasdaq and the Standard & Poor’s 500 both snapped a four-week streak of declines. MSCI’s (Morgan Stanley Capital International) gauge of stocks across the globe fell 4.15 points, or 0.37 percent, to 1,112.12.

The chip complex gave back Thursday’s bounce. Nvidia finished 2 percent lower and recorded a sixth straight weekly decline, its longest since October 2012. The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 3.4 percent. Advanced Micro Devices dropped 8.8 percent and Broadcom fell 4.8 percent, and Reuters named them the biggest drags on that index. The Standard & Poor’s 500 technology sector fell 1.7 percent, the worst of the 11 sectors. Microsoft ended 0.2 percent higher after a global outage of Outlook and Teams began to ease. Reuters said the company still had no timeline for full recovery. Data-centre names IREN lost 6.7 percent and Applied Digital fell 5.8 percent. Declining issues outnumbered advancers by 1.28 to 1 on the New York Stock Exchange and by 1.61 to 1 on the Nasdaq. The Standard & Poor’s 500 posted 9 new 52-week highs and 1 new low. The Nasdaq Composite recorded 37 new highs and 340 new lows. Volume was 16.38 billion shares, against a 20-day average of 18.52 billion.

In the final minutes the Federal Aviation Administration issued a national ground stop on departing flights, which it described as due to “extraordinary circumstance and reasons for safety” after reporting serious air-traffic-control communications problems. The NYSE Arca airline index closed down 1.4 percent.

The inflation print is Friday’s official number, and it did not take a December hike off the table. The Bureau of Labor Statistics said the Producer Price Index for final demand rose 0.3 percent in October, seasonally adjusted, after 1.4 percent in September and 0.4 percent in August. Economists polled by Reuters had forecast 0.4 percent. Reuters also said the September reading was revised up from 1.3 percent. On an unadjusted basis the index rose 6.5 percent over the 12 months to October, and the Bureau said that was the largest 12-month rise since 7.4 percent in November 2022. Goods rose 0.5 percent and services rose 0.2 percent, a fifth straight monthly rise in services. The index excluding foods, energy and trade services rose 0.5 percent on the month, the same as in September, and 4.9 percent on the year. Final-demand goods were up 10.2 percent on the year, the largest 12-month rise since 10.5 percent in October 2022. Reuters’ producer-price story said the Federal Reserve left its benchmark overnight rate in a 3.75 to 4.00 percent range last month, and that the government reported last week an unemployment rate of 4.1 percent, down from 4.2 percent in September.

Rate pricing moved around Friday afternoon, and the published snapshots are left as they were written. At 2:05 p.m. Eastern, Reuters’ gold story put the chance of a 25-basis-point December hike at 91.4 percent, down from 96.2 percent before the producer-price report, and the chance of a 50-basis-point hike at 8.6 percent, down from 13.8 percent, on the CME Group’s (Chicago Mercantile Exchange) FedWatch tool. The later technology closing story said a hike of at least 25 basis points at the December meeting was fully priced, with roughly a 10 percent chance of a 50-basis-point move. The equity closing wrap put that 50-basis-point chance at 11 percent for the 8–9 December meeting, against zero a week earlier. The 4:52 p.m. currency story put the chance of a 25-basis-point hike at roughly 90 percent. Those four readings are quoted as published. No Sunday FedWatch print was on these pages.

Long-term ETF lens

VWRA is the accumulating share class of Vanguard’s FTSE All-World UCITS ETF, ISIN IE00BK5BQT80, listed in francs on SIX. It does not distribute income. Vanguard’s ongoing charges figure, also called the total expense ratio, is 0.14 percent. The fund physically samples the FTSE All-World Index. The page read this morning still shows share-class assets of $62.19 billion and total assets of $88.84 billion. Outstanding shares were 329,991,065 as of 31 October 2026. As of 31 October the page shows a beta of 1.00, an R-squared of 1.00, and an annualised tracking error of 0.07 percent over one year, 0.07 percent over three years and 0.08 percent over five years.

The portfolio dates on the page are no longer all the same, and that is the one fund fact that moved between Saturday’s read and this one. The holdings table is now stamped 31 October 2026. Apple is the largest holding at 4.74363 percent of fund market value, then Nvidia at 4.54553 percent, Microsoft at 3.69160 percent, Amazon at 2.21951 percent and Taiwan Semiconductor at 1.68471 percent. Two Alphabet lines follow, at 1.58169 percent and 1.22962 percent. Broadcom is 1.44453 percent. The characteristics table, also as of 31 October, shows a price/earnings ratio of 19.6 times for the fund and the benchmark, a price/book ratio of 3.4 times, and a median market capitalisation of 187.3 billion for both. The stock count is still as of 30 September: 3,821 stocks in the fund against 4,199 in the index. Country weights are also still as of 30 September: United States 62.24 percent, Japan 6.06 percent, Taiwan 3.54 percent, United Kingdom 3.12 percent, Switzerland 1.88 percent.

Friday’s semiconductor selloff hits the second-largest published holding and another large technology weight, inside a fund that owns several thousand companies. Using the 31 October Nvidia weight of 4.54553 percent, a 2 percent decline in that holding is about 0.09 percentage point of the fund, before any other holding moves. That weight is the 31 October book. Friday’s live weight is a later number, and this page does not print it. The fund move is the net of every holding: the dollar net asset value fell 0.36 percent.

Friday’s dollar net asset value was $187.9772, shown on the page as $187.98, down $0.68, or 0.36 percent. The franc market price fell 1.02 francs, or 0.64 percent, to 157.16. Dividing Friday’s franc price by Friday’s net asset value gives about 0.8361 francs per dollar. Thursday’s pair gives about 0.8384. Those quotients are arithmetic on Vanguard’s prints. They are a market price divided by a net asset value, and they are different from the later New York dollar-franc quotes of 0.839 and 0.8394, which were published after the 17:30 Zurich close. Over a long holding period the franc price follows world equities and the franc’s exchange rate together. A higher Federal Reserve path raises the discount rate on a 19.6 times portfolio. A firmer dollar, if it persists into Monday’s Swiss session, cushions the franc quote on a day when the dollar net asset value falls. Friday’s SIX print recorded the equity decline. The evening currency prints came later. Monday is the next chance for either to show up in the franc price.

Hottest tactical

Thursday’s relative winners were Friday’s losers, and the weekend has not given that trade a new cash price. Advanced Micro Devices and Broadcom, which had risen with the first reaction to Nvidia’s leadership break, fell 8.8 percent and 4.8 percent. Nvidia itself lost another 2 percent and stretched its weekly losing streak to six. Europe, with less of the index in that trade, banked the strongest Euro Stoxx 50 week since April and a 2.1 percent week for the Swiss Market Index, then paused on Friday, with Nestlé and the insurers doing more of the holding than the chip names. Reuters’ week-ahead note says Anthropic’s listing is set to value the company at up to $1.4 trillion, with shares expected to start trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday, and that Nvidia reports on Tuesday under interim chief executive Colette Kress. Yonhap also described Kress, the chief financial officer, as interim chief executive, and quoted Daiwa Securities calling that a source of relief in Tokyo. For a fund that already owns Apple, Nvidia, Microsoft, Broadcom, the oil majors and the Swiss defensives at index weight, the tactical version of the next five sessions is a bet that this rotation keeps going through those two events. The listing, on the timetable Reuters described, is a sentiment event for that trade. It is a trading view on top of a holding that will add a new company only when the index does.

Biggest risk

The risk that reaches the whole fund is the discount rate, and it is unchanged by a quiet weekend. Producer prices rose less on the month than the Reuters poll, and 6.5 percent on the year, the largest 12-month rise since November 2022. The measure excluding foods, energy and trade services rose 0.5 percent for a second month. Closing stories still treated a December hike of at least 25 basis points as priced, with a live tail of a 50-basis-point move that different FedWatch snapshots put between 8.6 and 11 percent through Friday afternoon. The 10-year yield’s 3 p.m. close was 5.152 percent, up 0.294 percentage point on the week, the largest weekly rise since 11 April 2025 on Dow Jones’s telling. The dollar index was at its highest since April 2025. Gold’s roughly 4.5 percent weekly drop is the same story from the other side of the rate market. The next official cluster is retail sales, industrial production and housing this week, then personal income and the personal-consumption-expenditures deflator on 25 November, and the Federal Reserve meeting on 8–9 December.

The second risk is narrower and already inside the top of the book. Nvidia reports on Tuesday after the United States closing bell. The published 31 October weight is 4.55 percent, second to Apple, so a messy report hits a large position and a smaller share of the October book than a September table with Nvidia on top would have implied. The Financial Times reported that the board has approached Arm Holdings chief executive Rene Haas as a possible successor to Jensen Huang. Reuters reported that Huang stepped down after revelations of extramarital affairs, including a relationship with a businesswoman whom United States authorities suspect of spying for China. The suspicion is an allegation in that reporting. Reuters’ week-ahead note says President Donald Trump and President Xi Jinping are scheduled to meet on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation conference in Shenzhen on 18–19 November, and that Treasury secretary Scott Bessent said the leaders would advance an artificial-intelligence risk dialogue, with Nvidia chip sales to China in the background of that conversation. A difficult combination for this fund would be a discount rate that keeps rising and a messy Nvidia report in the same week as that meeting, because one hits the multiple on a 19.6 times portfolio and the other hits a top holding and the rules around what it can sell. The ground stop in United States aviation was a real Friday event. It is a smaller part of a global equity fund than the rate path or the top holdings.
