Americas

Reuters’ closing file (Caroline Valetkevitch and Ragini Mathur, carried by NBC News at 5:56 p.m. Eastern and by the Investing.com copy updated 8:30 p.m.) is the New York equity close. The Nasdaq Composite and the Standard & Poor’s 500 (S&P 500) finished lower. The Dow Jones Industrial Average finished higher, at 50,018.70.

  • S&P 500 7,201.09, down 36.08 points, or 0.50%.
  • Nasdaq Composite 23,733.54, down 356.02 points, or 1.48%.

Nvidia slipped 5.9%. Technology fell 2.8% and led the S&P 500 sector declines. An index of semiconductors dropped 7.9%. Healthcare rose and led the sector gainers; the close file gives no percentage for it. Retailers were among the day’s gainers: Abercrombie & Fitch rose 19.7% after raising its profit forecast, and Dollar Tree rose 11.2%, also after raising its outlook. The same Reuters file says Monday’s Nvidia-led jump had been the biggest daily percentage gain for the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq since April 2025. Tuesday gave part of that back. The political and personal allegations around Jensen Huang’s return remain allegations in the copy: the wire describes his resignation earlier this month after revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy, and says investors were weighing political and governance risks around the reinstatement. That is not a finding.

The Treasury story, a separate Reuters file, says the Wall Street Journal reported that the United States was preparing to raise tariffs on China and other countries. Neither file is a published tariff schedule. The afternoon bond copy and the equity-close copy do not use the same list of countries. They are not merged here.

The Conference Board’s consumer confidence index, in the Reuters Treasury story (Chuck Mikolajczak, “updates to afternoon trading”), jumped to 103.3 in November, the highest since January 2025, from a slightly upwardly revised 91.9 in October. Economists polled by Reuters had looked for 92.5. The equity-close file describes the same survey only as the highest in nearly two years, with no index level.

Long-term ETF lens

VWRA is the accumulating Vanguard FTSE All-World UCITS ETF, priced in francs on SIX, ISIN IE00BK5BQT80. It is one global equity fund. The published country weights are still as at 31 October: United States 62.83%, Japan 5.94%, Taiwan 3.31%, United Kingdom 3.25%, Switzerland 2.08%. The fund held 3,574 stocks against 4,199 in the FTSE All-World Index. The price/earnings ratio was 19.6 times. The three largest holdings were still Apple 4.74363%, Nvidia 4.54553% and Microsoft 3.69160%. Share-class assets were $62.19 billion and total assets $88.84 billion. The ongoing charges figure is 0.14%. None of those portfolio figures moved on the page read this morning. They do not reflect November’s swing in the artificial-intelligence names.

Tuesday’s franc price and the dollar net asset value moved in opposite directions. That is the point for a holder who lives in francs. A single global fund can fall in the currency of the savings account on a day the fund’s dollar value rises. Over the ten rows Vanguard is still showing, the franc price is also down by more than the dollar net asset value, but that is a separate arithmetic span, not Tuesday’s session. See the VWRA section for both.

For a long horizon, Tuesday is a fade of one rebound inside a fund that is still mostly American equities, with a thin Swiss sleeve. The 31 October weights mean the fund, as last disclosed, is more exposed to the companies that led both Monday’s jump and Tuesday’s give-back than a domestic Swiss portfolio is. The SMI itself was unchanged. VWRA in francs was not.

Hottest tactical

The heat is the gap between the order book and the tape. Dell Technologies reported after the Tuesday close. The company release: record quarterly revenue of $54.9 billion, up 103%; record non-GAAP diluted earnings per share of $9.45, up 265%; Infrastructure Solutions Group revenue of $42.0 billion, up 198%, including $22.0 billion of artificial-intelligence-optimised servers. Artificial-intelligence server backlog ended at a record $132.3 billion after $60.2 billion of orders. Full-year revenue guidance went to $202.0 billion from $192.0 billion. The additional buyback authorisation is $25.0 billion. Reuters’ copy prints quarterly revenue as $54.94 billion, against an LSEG estimate of $49.32 billion, and adjusted earnings per share of $9.45 against an estimate of $6.59. Reuters said the shares were up about 5% in extended trading. Mandia’s European-close file, written before those results, had Dell down more than 12% on the session. The regular-session closing percentage was not in the New York equity-close story. Reuters also said Dell and HP both warned that the memory-chip shortage runs into 2027. HP’s fourth-quarter revenue, in that Reuters story only, was $16 billion against an estimate of $15.42 billion.

That fundamental print did not stop Tuesday’s cash market from handing back a large piece of Monday. Nvidia’s close was −5.9%, semiconductors −7.9%, and Tokyo −2.78% the next morning. For a fund that still lists Nvidia as its second holding on stale October weights, the tactical question is whether the spending Dell just booked keeps showing up in index earnings, or whether the multiple keeps being marked down faster than the orders arrive. That is a description of the tension. It is not a trade.

Biggest risk

Today’s United States inflation print meets a rates market that has already gone a long way toward a December hike, plus a tariff story that is still a report. October personal income, personal spending and the Personal Consumption Expenditures price index are due on Wednesday. The equity-close file calls that index one of the gauges the Federal Reserve watches. FedWatch in that file was at 97% for a December hike and 66% for another move in January. The afternoon bond file had December fully priced for at least 25 basis points and only a 2.9% chance of 50. A hotter price index would land on a market that is not starting from an easy-policy assumption.

The tariff risk is the one that is not yet a document. New duties, if the reported probe is released as the wire describes, would push on goods prices while the central bank is being priced to tighten. VWRA’s last published United States weight is 62.83%. The second channel is the franc. Tuesday already showed it: the dollar net asset value rose 0.24% and the franc market price fell 2.18%. A firmer franc can cut the balance the saver sees even when the global index in dollars does not.
