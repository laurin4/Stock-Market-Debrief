Americas

Reuters correspondent Rodrigo Campos, in the 22:48 file carried on Euronext, reported the Wednesday New York close: the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1.11% to 50,198.17, the Standard & Poor’s 500 lost 0.23% to 7,323.63, and the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.42% to 24,429.89. MSCI’s gauge of stocks across the globe fell 0.45% to 1,074.61. That Standard & Poor’s decline extends the slide that Tuesday’s close had already stretched to four straight sessions, the first such streak since September. This morning’s wires did not themselves print the words “fifth day.”

Nvidia fell 2.9% after rising as much as 2.7% on the day. Campos wrote that the board dismissed the idea of rehiring former chief executive Jensen Huang, who resigned last week, and that the results and outlook did not dispel the uncertainty. A Yonhap television script on Thursday morning put the same share move at 2.89%. Those two prints are left side by side.

An earlier Campos write-through, still online and timestamped 19:33, is not the close. It had the Dow down 0.84% at 50,334.82, the Standard & Poor’s 500 down 0.07% at 7,335.43 and the Nasdaq up 0.46% at 24,439.22. Those running marks are omitted from the figures above.

In that same 22:48 file the dollar index rose 0.17% to 103.79, a fresh 19-month high, and the euro was down 0.22% at $1.0973. The 2-year Treasury yield rose 1.31 basis points to 4.802%, from 4.789% late Tuesday. The 10-year yield fell 3.47 basis points to 5.068%, from 5.103%. The story does not label the 10-year as the 15:00 Tradeweb close. The 19:33 file had a different running 10-year, 5.064%, and a 30-year yield of 5.414%, down 4.49 basis points from 5.459%. The later file did not repeat the 30-year.

The Federal Reserve’s minutes of the 27–28 October meeting, released Wednesday, said “almost all” policymakers agreed further rate increases would be needed if the economy evolved as they expected. A few said inflation could require a more restrictive stance than currently expected. A couple said the Fed might have to resort to a half-percentage-point increase “if incoming inflation data were unfavorable.” Reuters correspondent Howard Schneider wrote that recent releases had met that benchmark. The committee held the policy rate at 3.75% to 4.00%. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed president Beth Hammack dissented in favour of a quarter-point increase. CNBC, citing the minutes, said staff had called demand from artificial intelligence “a material source of inflation risk,” and that off-balance-sheet leasing and circular financing among related firms could amplify losses if the investment returns disappointed. CNBC also reported October consumer prices up 0.4% on the headline index and 0.3% excluding food and energy, each a tenth of a point above the expectation it cited.

Governor Christopher Waller told CNBC the central bank should either approve a half-point increase at the 8–9 December meeting or communicate a faster pace of increases in 2027, including language that rates would rise again in January. He said recent inflation was running above 3% annualised and that firms were starting to treat 3% as a normal price increase. Schneider’s minutes story says investors anticipate a quarter-point increase next month, without a probability. CNBC’s reading of the CME Group FedWatch gauge was that markets were fully pricing a quarter-point move at the December meeting, with low odds of a half-point, and a solid likelihood of two further increases in 2027. No numeric FedWatch percentage is printed here, because the copies read this morning did not give one.

Long-term exchange-traded fund lens

The holding in view is the accumulating Vanguard FTSE All-World UCITS ETF, an Irish undertaking for collective investment in transferable securities that tracks the FTSE All-World Index by owning shares. Swiss ticker VWRA, ISIN IE00BK5BQT80, ongoing charges 0.14%. On the Vanguard Switzerland page this morning the Wednesday SIX close was 153.06 francs, up 0.32 francs, or 0.21%. The dollar net asset value was $181.7240, down $0.84, or 0.46%. The page rounds those to 153.06 francs and $181.72. The price and net-asset-value stamps both read “at closure 18 Nov 2026.”

The franc price rose while the dollar net asset value fell. That is what a weaker franc does to a dollar fund on a day when the equity move is modest. It is not a sign that the underlying portfolio had a good Wednesday. AWP’s evening dollar, 0.8439 francs, was the local currency fact sitting behind that gap. A stronger franc from here would stop cushioning the Swiss price.

The 52-week market high and low on the page were unchanged at 162.38 and 129.36 francs. The stamp on that range had moved to “at closure 19 Nov 2026.” From those two printed levels, 153.06 francs is about 5.7% under the high and about 18.3% above the low. The dollar net-asset-value range on the same stamp was $195.62 to $161.35.

Portfolio data are still as at 31 October 2026. The fund held 3,574 stocks against 4,199 in the benchmark. The price-to-earnings ratio was 19.6 times. The United States was 62.83% of the fund (benchmark 62.71%). Switzerland was 2.08%. The largest holdings were Apple 4.74363%, Nvidia 4.54553% and Microsoft 3.69160%. A one-day argument about a single initial public offering does not rebalance that book. What does reach it is a United States rate path, because almost two-thirds of the fund is American equity, plus the dollar against the franc, because the share is priced in francs on SIX.

Hottest tactical

Anthropic priced its offering on Wednesday at $83 a share, above the $70 to $78 range, and is due to start trading on Nasdaq today under the ticker ANT. Reuters, citing the filings, said the company sold about 19% more shares than first planned, 980 million shares, raising $81.3 billion. Including a $26 billion private placement at the same price, the deal is $107.3 billion. Fully diluted, Reuters put the value at about $1.5 trillion. Without dilution, about $1.1 trillion. It is the largest stock-market debut on record in that account, ahead of SpaceX in June.

The filing figures Reuters reported are part of why the session matters beyond the first trade. Annualised revenue was $81 billion as of 31 October, up $10 billion from 30 September. Third-quarter revenue was $16.96 billion, up 47% from the second quarter. The operating result swung to a loss of $567 million from operating income of $974 million. Computing spend was nearly $9 billion in the first half and another $5 billion in the third quarter. Commitments for computing with infrastructure partners, including Amazon and Alphabet, were nearly $900 billion. The filing warned that if partner financing fails, contracted capacity may be delayed, repriced, reduced or not delivered.

For a fund that already owns the listed suppliers and customers of that build-out, the tactical point is the test, not a ticket in the offering. Nvidia’s drop after a strong report showed that good numbers are not, this week, enough. Marex’s Alexis Bert, in the Reuters Europe story, said the amount being raised could drain liquidity from the rest of the market. AWP’s Swiss close said some investors were talking about selling other artificial-intelligence names to take part. Those are market comments, not flows.

Biggest risk

The risk that reaches a long-term all-world holder is the December Federal Reserve meeting, not the opening print of one new stock. A sitting governor has put a half-point increase on the table for 8–9 December, and the October minutes show a couple of participants already discussing that step if inflation data stayed unfavourable. Schneider wrote that the recent data had met the benchmark they described. The fund is 62.83% United States. A rate shock hits that book directly, in the dollar net asset value, whether or not the franc softens enough to hide it in the Swiss price.

The same minutes, in CNBC’s reading, treat the artificial-intelligence financing chain as an inflation source and as a financial-stability issue: debt growing through complex structures, exposure concentrated in a few firms, and circular financing that could amplify losses. That sits inside this fund through Nvidia, Broadcom, Taiwan Semiconductor, Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet and Meta. Wednesday’s 4.5% drop in copper, and Europe’s 4.7% drop in miners, is the other face of the same tape: the growth-sensitive slice can reprice in a day when the dollar and real yields move.
