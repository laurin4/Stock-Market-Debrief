Long-term ETF lens

VWRA is one accumulating share class of the Vanguard FTSE All-World UCITS ETF, an Undertaking for Collective Investment in Transferable Securities, listed in francs on SIX. It is built to track the FTSE All-World Index by owning a representative sample of the large and mid-sized companies in that index. The ongoing charge is 0.14%. It does not pay a dividend; the income stays inside the fund. A one-day drop in the franc price does not change that structure.

What did change on Wednesday is the currency gap inside the same fund. The dollar net asset value fell 0.67%, from a header price of $177.56. The history row is $177.5639, down $1.20. The SIX price fell 2.55%, or 3.78 francs, to 144.56. Both stamps read “at closure 25 Nov 2026.” On Tuesday the franc price and the dollar net asset value had moved in opposite directions. On Wednesday they fell together, and the franc price fell by more. That is the Swiss-franc translation of a global equity fund on a day when the dollar was softer against the franc in the currency stories above, not a change in the holdings list.

Holdings and country weights are still as at 31 October. Apple was 4.74363% of the fund, Nvidia 4.54553%, Microsoft 3.69160%. The United States was 62.83%. Switzerland was 2.08%. The fund held 3,574 stocks against 4,199 in the benchmark. The price-to-earnings ratio was 19.6 times. Share-class assets were $62.19 billion and total assets $88.84 billion, both still dated 31 October on the page. The 52-week franc high and low are unchanged at 162.38 and 129.36, and the net-asset-value range is unchanged at $195.62 and $161.35. The stamp on those ranges has moved to “at closure 26 Nov 2026,” while the price itself is still Wednesday’s.

For a holder who is not trading the week, Wednesday was another mark lower in a fund that is still a global equity basket, still mostly American shares, and still above both franc alerts. The distance that matters this morning is 4.56 francs, not the holiday bounce in futures.

Biggest risk

VWRA at 144.56 francs is 4.56 francs, or about 3.2% of the price, above 140, and 9.56 francs above 135. Neither alert was hit. The 52-week high of 162.38 is 17.82 francs above Wednesday’s close, which is 11.0% of that high, an arithmetic gap and not a Vanguard statistic. A further rise in the franc against the dollar would close more of the gap to 140 even if the dollar net asset value only drifts. The fund is still 62.83% United States and 4.55% Nvidia on the 31 October holdings, Nvidia fell another 3.9% on Wednesday, and a ratings watch is now part of that story. December is priced for a Federal Reserve increase, on the two FedWatch clocks above, with core PCE at 3.3% on the year. The holiday bounce does not retire that combination.
